\section{Localized defects}\label{sec:defects}

\Cref{asm:closure} posits a pressureless product carrying a fraction $\bfrac$
of the converted energy. This section asks what such an object could be. The
answer offered is a topological texture, and the section is careful about what
is and is not established: a finite-energy solution with an integer charge and
a preferred size is exhibited and solved, but its identification with dark
matter remains a conjecture, and its abundance is not computed.

\subsection{An obstruction, and how to evade it}\label{sec:defects-derrick}

\begin{proposition}[Derrick scaling]\label{prop:derrick}
  In three spatial dimensions let $E[\phi]=E_2+E_0$ with
  $E_2=\tfrac12\int|\nabla\phi|^2$ and $E_0=\int W(\phi)$, both finite and
  $W\ge0$ after subtracting the vacuum. Under $\phi_\sigma(\mathbf
  x)=\phi(\sigma\mathbf x)$ one has $E(\sigma)=\sigma^{-1}E_2+\sigma^{-3}E_0$,
  so a non-trivial stationary point would require $E_2+3E_0=0$, impossible for
  $E_2>0$.
\end{proposition}

A single static canonical scalar therefore has no non-trivial static localized
solution at all, which is stronger than the absence of a stable one and is what
is needed here.
The hypotheses are what matter: time dependence, a conserved charge, gauge
fields, gravity, or additional terms in the energy each evade the conclusion.
We take the last route. Let $n^A(\mathbf x)$, $A=1,\dots,4$, satisfy
$n^An^A=1$ with a constant value at infinity, so that configurations are maps
$S^3\to S^3$, and take
%
\begin{keyresult}{A}{eq:defects-energy}
  E=\int \dd^3x\left[\frac{\Fstar^2}{2}\partial_i n^A\partial_i n^A
  +\frac{\kaps}{4}\mathcal W_{ij}^{AB}\mathcal W_{ij}^{AB}\right],
  \qquad
  \mathcal W_{ij}^{AB}=\partial_i n^A\partial_j n^B-\partial_j n^A\partial_i n^B ,
\end{keyresult}
%
with $\Fstar>0$ of mass dimension one and $\kaps>0$ dimensionless. Under the
same scaling the two terms go as $\sigma^{-1}$ and $\sigma^{+1}$, so
\cref{prop:derrick} no longer applies and a preferred size exists. This is the
Skyrme mechanism, and \cref{eq:defects-energy} is the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Skyrme
energy with $F_\pi^2=4\Fstar^2$ and $e^2=1/(2\kaps)$; we use it as a
calculable representative of a localized product, not as a new proposal.

\subsection{Degree, bound, and the trial family}\label{sec:defects-trial}

The topological degree
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defects-degree}
  \degB=\frac{1}{12\pi^2}\int \dd^3x\,\epsilon^{ijk}\epsilon_{ABCD}
  n^A\partial_i n^B\partial_j n^C\partial_k n^D
\end{equation}
%
is an integer on such maps. A pointwise application of the arithmetic-geometric
mean inequality to the singular values of $\dd n$ gives the Bogomolny-type
bound
%
\begin{keyresult}{I}{eq:defects-bound}
  E\ \ge\ 6\sqrt2\,\pi^2\,\Fstar\sqrt{\kaps}\,\abs{\degB} .
\end{keyresult}

For the rational-map family $n^a=2\Rs x^a/(r^2+\Rs^2)$,
$n^4=(r^2-\Rs^2)/(r^2+\Rs^2)$, direct quadrature gives $\degB=1$ and
%
\begin{keyresult}{I}{eq:defects-trial}
  E(\Rs)=6\pi^2\left(\Fstar^2\Rs+\frac{\kaps}{\Rs}\right),
  \qquad
  \Rs^{*}=\frac{\sqrt{\kaps}}{\Fstar},
  \qquad
  E_{\rm trial}=12\pi^2\Fstar\sqrt{\kaps} .
\end{keyresult}

\subsection{The solution}\label{sec:defects-solution}

The draft stopped at \cref{eq:defects-trial}, which is only an upper bound on
the infimum of the sector. Solving the field equations is straightforward and
sharpens the statement. With the hedgehog ansatz $n^4=\cos F(r)$,
$n^a=\sin F(r)\,\hat x^a$ and $r=(\sqrt{\kaps}/\Fstar)x$, the Euler--Lagrange
equation of \cref{eq:defects-energy} is
%
\begin{keyresult}{I}{eq:defects-ode}
  \left(x^2+4\sin^2F\right)F''+2xF'+2\sin 2F\,F'^2
  -\sin 2F-\frac{2\sin 2F\sin^2F}{x^2}=0 ,
\end{keyresult}
%
with $F(0)=\pi$ and $F(\infty)=0$, regular series $F\simeq\pi-\kappa_1 x$ at
the origin and tail $F\simeq C/x^2$. Solving this boundary-value problem
numerically (\cref{app:numerics}) gives
%
\begin{keyresult}{N}{eq:defects-solved}
  E_{\degB=1}=\numSkyrmeEnergy\,\Fstar\sqrt{\kaps}
  =\numSkyrmeBoundRatio\times\left(6\sqrt2\pi^2\Fstar\sqrt{\kaps}\right) ,
\end{keyresult}
%
which is \numSkyrmeTrialRatio\ of the trial value \cref{eq:defects-trial}, and
which satisfies the virial identity $E_2=E_4$ to machine precision and returns
$\degB=1$ to quadrature accuracy. The trial family therefore overestimates the
energy by about thirteen per cent, and the solved configuration sits above the
Bogomolny bound by the factor familiar from the Skyrme model.

\paragraph{Limits of this calculation.} A minimum within the hedgehog ansatz is
not a proof of stability against all deformations: that would require the full
Hessian. And the energy of a solution is not a particle mass until the sector
is quantised; the collective coordinates, their moments of inertia, and the
Finkelstein--Rubinstein constraints all enter. What
\cref{eq:defects-solved} establishes is that the localized sector of
\cref{asm:closure} has a concrete, calculable representative with an integer
charge, a finite energy and a preferred size.

\subsection{Why the equation of state is what it is}\label{sec:defects-eos}

Being localized is not by itself enough to be pressureless. For an isotropic
population of objects of mass $m_D$ with distribution $\mathcal F(\mathbf p)$,
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defects-eos}
  \rhoD=\int \dd^3p\,E_{\mathbf p}\mathcal F,
  \qquad
  p_D=\frac13\int \dd^3p\,\frac{\mathbf p^2}{E_{\mathbf p}}\mathcal F,
  \qquad
  \frac{p_D}{\rhoD}\simeq\frac{\avg{v^2}}{3}\ \ll1
  \ \text{ iff }\ \abs{\mathbf p}\ll m_D .
\end{equation}
%
So the pressureless assumption is a statement about the momentum distribution
of the products, and formation by absorption could well produce fast objects.
That the conversion is the same process which sets the momentum distribution is
a constraint the model does not currently compute.

Dimensionality matters as much as localization. For a frozen comoving
population of defects of spatial dimension $d$ with energy proportional to
their extent, $\rho\propto a^{d-3}$ and hence $w=-d/3$: point-like relics give
$w=0$, frozen strings $-1/3$, frozen walls $-2/3$. A $U(1)$ vortex in three
dimensions is a string, not a particle, and a string's energy per unit length
being finite does not make an infinite string's energy finite. The texture
above is genuinely three-dimensional and point-like, which is why it is the
representative chosen here.

\subsection{Caustics: what conversion can and cannot do}
\label{sec:defects-caustics}

The clock congruence of \cref{sec:realizations-clock} is pressureless and
geodesic, so it forms caustics, and it is natural to ask whether conversion
regularizes them: the transfer $\QV=(1-\bfrac)\Gam\rhoI$ diverges where $\rhoI$
does, which looks like a runaway sink. An earlier version of this work
conjectured that it was. It is not, and the reason is worth stating because it
is elementary and general.

Along a flow line with proper time $\tau$ and expansion $\expa$,
%
\begin{keyresult}{I}{eq:defects-local-exact}
  \rhoI(\tau)=\rhoI(\tau_0)\,
  \exp\left[-\int_{\tau_0}^{\tau}\left(\expa+\Gam\right)\dd s\right] .
\end{keyresult}
%
Conversion enters only through the \emph{integral} of the rate.

\begin{theorem}[Bounded rates cannot regularize a caustic]
  \label{thm:bounded-rate-obstruction}
  Let $0\le\Gam\le G<\infty$ on $[\tau_0,T)$ with $T-\tau_0$ finite, and let
  the compression be non-integrable, $\int^{T}\expa\,\dd s=-\infty$. Then
  $\rhoI\to\infty$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  $\int^{\tau}\Gam\,\dd s\le G(T-\tau_0)<\infty$, so the conversion factor lies
  in $[e^{-G(T-\tau_0)},1]$ and is bounded below by a positive constant, while
  the compression factor diverges.
\end{proof}

Under \cref{asm:activation} the rate obeys $\Gam\le\Gamz e^{\alpha+\beta}$, so
\cref{thm:bounded-rate-obstruction} applies to the model as it stands. The
error in the earlier conjecture was to confuse the diverging transfer per unit
volume, $\QV$, with the fractional loss rate $\Gam$; only the latter appears in
\cref{eq:defects-local-exact}, and only it is bounded.

\paragraph{What a compression-driven rate would have to do.} Take
$\expa=-c/(T-t)$ and $\Gam=g/(T-t)$ plus a bounded part. Then
$\rhoI\sim(T-t)^{-(c-g)}$, and three separate conditions must hold, not one.
The precursor is finite for $g\ge c$. The vacuum increment integrates
$(T-t)^{g-c-1}$ and therefore needs $g>c$ \emph{strictly}: at $g=c$ the
precursor is finite while the vacuum still diverges logarithmically. And the
defect density obeys $\dot\rhoD=-\expa\rhoD+\bfrac\Gam\rhoI$. Its homogeneous
part is $\rhoD(t_0)\abs{J_0/J}$, which diverges as $(T-t)^{-c}$ for \emph{any}
non-zero initial defect density, whatever $\bfrac$ is: the localized product is
pressureless dust and is compressed by the same $\expa$ as everything else.
Setting $\bfrac=0$ removes the source, not that. Regularity therefore requires
both that none be produced during the collapse and that none be present when it
begins. Its column density stays finite either way, which is the distinction
between a finite mass and a finite density. Hence

\begin{keyresult}{C}{eq:defects-regularity}
  \begin{gathered}
    \text{for }\expa=-c/(T-t),\ \Gam=g/(T-t)+\text{bounded},\\
    \text{along a geodesic pressureless congruence:}\\[2pt]
    T_{\mu\nu}^{\rm (dark)}\ \text{finite at the caustic}
    \iff g>c\ \ \text{and}\ \ \bfrac=0\ \ \text{and}\ \ \rhoD(t_0)=0 .
  \end{gathered}
\end{keyresult}

The hypotheses are displayed with the statement because without them it reads
as a theorem about caustics in general, which it is not: it is a statement
about the self-similar fold profile above, and $T_{\mu\nu}$ means the dark
sector's own stress.

It is worth being careful about what that buys. Pointwise finiteness of the
stress tensor is not a criterion for the absence of a spacetime singularity,
and a generic fold caustic of a dust congruence is not a strong curvature
singularity in the sense of Tipler
\parencite{tipler1977_singularities,clarke1985_strongcurvature}: the double integral of
$R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu$ along a causal geodesic converges, and the spacetime is
expected to be extendible through it. What the conditions above control is
therefore the divergence of the source in the field equations, not the
existence of a genuine singularity. That makes the result useful for deciding
whether the effective description of \cref{asm:closure} remains applicable, and
not for any claim about singularity avoidance.

For $\Gam_{\rm collapse}=\kappa\,[-\expa]_+$ one has $g=\kappa c$, so the
condition is the dimensionless statement $\kappa>1$, independent of the
strength of the collapse; for a generic Zel'dovich pancake $c=1$.

The requirement sits awkwardly with the rest of this section, and the tension
should be named rather than left for the reader. A finite relic abundance of
defects needs $\bfrac>0$ (\cref{sec:defects-abundance}); regularity at a
caustic needs $\bfrac=0$, and the third condition of
\cref{eq:defects-regularity} makes the conflict sharper than a statement about
$\bfrac$ alone. One might hope to separate the two channels --- form the
defects earlier, by some other mechanism, and let the compression-driven
channel feed only the vacuum --- but that does not help: defects already
present on the collapsing sheet are pressureless dust, are compressed by the
same $\expa$, and carry the divergence by themselves. Pointwise regularity of
$T_{\mu\nu}$ at a fold caustic is therefore incompatible with the presence of
\emph{any} localized product there, however it was made. Either the localized
sector acquires pressure, in which case it is no longer the pressureless
product of \cref{asm:closure} and its equation of state must be recomputed, or
the collapsing sheets are free of defects for a reason this paper does not
supply. We record this as an unresolved tension.

\paragraph{The covariant price.} Adding such a channel is not free.
\Cref{eq:setup-vacuum-transfer} forces the transfer four-vector to be closed,
and for a geodesic clock that means the transfer must be constant on comoving
slices, which a rate depending on $\expa$ is not, precisely at a caustic.
Transferring instead to a second pressureless sector is covariantly
unconstrained, because two dust sectors sharing a congruence exchange energy
with no integrability condition, but then the total dust obeys the unmodified
continuity equation and the divergence is relocated rather than weakened.
Keeping the exact vacuum requires a momentum transfer $q^\nu=-D^\nu \rhoV$,
which makes the dust non-geodesic and whose force density itself diverges at
the caustic. The sector that can absorb the divergence is the one that does not
dilute, and that is exactly the sector whose transfer is constrained.

One further option is worth recording because it is the least damaging and is
easy to miss. Give the receiver a traceless spatial anisotropic stress
$\Pi^{\mu\nu}$ alongside the vacuum. Total conservation with a geodesic dust
then requires only $\covd_\mu\Pi^{\mu i}=D^i \rhoV$, whose isotropic part is
unchanged, so the equation of state stays $p=-\rhoV$; and $\Pi$ vanishes
identically both in FLRW, by isotropy, and at linear order under
\cref{asm:geodesic}, because $\delta \rhoV_{\rm com}=0$ there. It switches on
only where the vacuum fails to be comoving-uniform. This is not a repair,
however, and the size of the gap can be stated exactly: a symmetric traceless
spatial tensor has five independent components and the conservation condition
is three equations, so a closure must supply two further relations per point.
Until it does, the system is underdetermined --- $\Pi$ is fixed only up to a
divergence-free traceless tensor --- and what the option achieves is to replace
an obstruction by a counted deficit. Supplying such a closure is left open.

\paragraph{What is not disturbed, and in what sense.} The candidate channel
$\kappa[-\expa]_+$ vanishes identically on the open set $\{\expa>0\}$. An
expanding background has $\expa=3\Hub>0$, so the background is untouched, and
so is any perturbation small enough in the supremum norm that $\expa$ stays
positive everywhere: for such a perturbation every order of the expansion
vanishes, not merely the linear one. Nothing in
\cref{sec:homogeneous-cosmology,sec:cosmography,sec:perturbations,sec:observations}
changes.

The qualification is not decorative. The statement is pointwise, and the
topology has to be $L^\infty$: in $L^p$ with $p<\infty$ a perturbation can keep
its amplitude while shrinking its support, so it is small in norm and still
drives $\expa$ negative somewhere. The statement likewise fails for any
coarse-grained description in which $\expa$ has a distribution with support
below zero. If $\expa$ is Gaussian about $3\Hub$ with width $\sigma$, then
$\avg{[-\expa]_+}$ is positive for every $\sigma$ and is suppressed only as
$e^{-9\Hub^2/2\sigma^2}$: non-perturbative rather than zero, and numerically
$8.3\times10^{-2}$ of $3\Hub$ at $\sigma=3\Hub$ but $7.4\times10^{-26}$ at
$\sigma=0.3\Hub$. So once structure formation has produced collapsing regions,
an averaged treatment of this channel is not exactly zero; what is exactly zero
is its contribution wherever the local expansion is positive, which is what the
observational sections use.

\paragraph{A rate proportional to the expansion has no positive local
extension.} The solvable family of \cref{sec:solvable-family} uses
$\Gam=\xi_0\Hub$. Covariantizing it in the obvious way, $\Hub\to\expa/3$, gives
a rate that is negative wherever $\expa<0$, which violates the non-negativity
assumed throughout and in particular in \cref{prop:survival}. That family
therefore has no unambiguous local extension into a collapsing region, and the
kinetic law it belongs to has to be read as a statement about the homogeneous
mode, as \cref{asm:geodesic} already requires.

\paragraph{Numerical test, and what it does not cover.}
\Cref{fig:pancake} reports a one-dimensional Zel'dovich pancake carrying the
local sector along Lagrangian sheets. Three limitations should be stated before
the numbers. The trajectories are prescribed by the Zel'dovich map, so the test
isolates the effect of conversion from the gravitational back-reaction and is
not a self-consistent solution. The runs approach the caustic but do not cross
it: with the fiducial profile the Jacobian is $J=1-D\cos kq>0$ for $D<1$, so
the caustic sits at $D=1$ and the multi-stream region beyond it is untested.
And, most importantly for the mechanism, after a crossing the Jacobian grows
again, so $\expa$ turns positive and $\kappa[-\expa]_+$ \emph{switches itself
off} exactly where it was meant to act. What the compression-driven channel can
do, it must therefore do on the approach; the integrated hazard
$\kappa\log(1/\abs{J})$ does diverge there, which is why the exponents below are
well defined, but nothing here describes the multi-stream regime.

A fourth limitation is one of measurement rather than of physics. The sheets
are discrete, so the one nearest the caustic sits at $q=\Delta q/2$ and its
Jacobian bottoms out at $J=s+(1-s)\varepsilon$ with
$\varepsilon=1-\cos(k\Delta q/2)\simeq(k\Delta q/2)^2/2$. That floor, not the
time integration, is what limits how close to the caustic an exponent can be
read: at the resolution used here it is $\varepsilon\simeq2\times10^{-8}$, and
a window at $s\sim10^{-5}$ therefore returns an exponent a few parts in $10^4$
short of its limit. The run with no conversion is best read as a check of the
solver against the exact kinematic result $\rhoI=1/J$, which it reproduces to a
relative error of $5\times10^{-11}$; what the other runs add is the behaviour of
the coupled system, where the compression channel acts together with the
bounded reinforcing rate and its memory, and where the exponent is consequently
not available in closed form.

The measured exponents reproduce the analytic values. With no conversion the
central density follows $s^{\numPancakeANoneExpRhoINear}$, and with the bounded
rate of \cref{eq:process-rate} it follows $s^{\numPancakeBCosmoExpRhoINear}$:
the same exponent, with the amplitude lowered by a finite factor of
$\numPancakeSummaryBoundedSuppression$. That is
\cref{thm:bounded-rate-obstruction} made visible --- a bounded rate buys an
amplitude, never an exponent. The compression-driven channel gives $\kappa-1$,
measured as $\numPancakeCCollapseKZpVExpRhoINear$,
$\numPancakeCCollapseKOpZExpRhoINear$,
$\numPancakeCCollapseKOpVExpRhoINear$ and
$\numPancakeCCollapseKWpZExpRhoINear$ for $\kappa=0.5$, $1$, $1.5$ and $3$
against the predicted $-0.5$, $0$, $0.5$ and $2$, and the exponent is
independent of the branching ratio, the memory time and the reinforcement
strengths --- asymptotically, and measurably so: the spread of the fitted
exponent across those choices falls by about a decade for each decade closer
to the caustic. Across all $\numPancakeSummaryNCases$ runs the largest
violation of the three identities the simulation conserves exactly ---
$\rhoI\abs{J}=1-\fconv$, $\rhoD\abs{J}=\bfrac\fconv$, and their sum ---
is $\numPancakeSummaryWorstIdentityResidual$.

\subsection{What would be needed for an abundance}\label{sec:defects-abundance}

By \cref{eq:cosmology-survival}, $\rhoD a^3\to\rho_{D,\rm act}+\bfrac\rho_{I,\rm act}$,
so the comoving abundance is fixed by the branching ratio and the initial
data. Converting that into a number density requires the mass $m_D$, which the
background does not constrain, and a viable relic further requires either an
asymmetry or a production rate low enough that annihilation is negligible,
together with a free-streaming length short enough not to erase structure. None
of this is computed here. Where such a computation has been carried through for
a Skyrme-type soliton --- in the electroweak sector rather than here --- the
constraints are severe and instructive
\parencite{kitano2016_160507355}; nothing in this paper is close to that
standard, and the comparison is the honest measure of the distance. The topological charge is also not a spin: although
$\pi_4(S^3)=\Int_2$ permits a Finkelstein--Rubinstein quantisation in which the
soliton is a fermion, establishing it requires showing that the $2\pi$ rotation
loop is non-contractible in the configuration space and choosing a
representation of its fundamental group. \Cref{sec:outlook} lists both as open.

\paragraph{What this section establishes.}
A solved, finite-energy, unit-charge configuration with a preferred size, and
the exact conditions under which such a population is pressureless. What it
does not establish is that these objects exist, that they are stable against
general deformations, that they are fermions, or that their relic abundance
matches the observed dark matter.
