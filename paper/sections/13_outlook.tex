\section{Outlook: open directions}\label{sec:outlook}

Everything in this section is conjecture. It is separated from the rest of the
paper for that reason, and each item is stated with what would have to be shown
for it to become a result. The framework of the preceding sections stands or
falls independently of anything here.

\subsection{Where the rate law would come from}\label{sec:outlook-rate}

The reinforced rate \cref{eq:process-rate} and the activation law
\cref{eq:solvable-activation} are effective descriptions with five parameters.
\Cref{sec:realizations-bridge} proposed that a nucleation event in a four-form
sector is an absorption event. Part of that proposal is now a calculation
rather than a conjecture, and it is worth being exact about which part.

The reinforcement is derived: \cref{eq:realizations-lnrate,eq:realizations-alpha}
give $\log\Gam_\uparrow$ and fix $\alpha=3(N\mathsf q/h)\Delta S$, with the
range of validity of the exponential law and its truncation error, under the
thin-wall and detailed-balance hypotheses stated there. What those hypotheses
do not cover is the one the mechanism needs most: detailed balance is an
equilibrium relation, and the nucleation here is assumed to be driven by the
precursor rather than thermal. Redoing the calculation for a driven process is
the open problem, and it is the same computation that would supply the
prefactor and convert a rate per four-volume into a rate per correlated region.
That last conversion would also decide whether the memory time $\tauM$ is the
light-crossing time of that region, which remains the only physical candidate
we can point to.

What is not derived, and is now known not to follow from this bridge, is the
identification of $\bfrac$ with a fraction ending in localized objects. By
\cref{rem:bridge-limits} the membrane leaves no stable remnant in this action,
and the object of \cref{sec:defects} is built from different fields carrying a
different charge. The conjecture that survives is therefore narrower:
%
\begin{keyresult}{S}{eq:outlook-bridge}
  \text{the driven rate retains}\quad
  \odv{\log\Gam}{h}>0
  \quad\text{with}\quad
  \bfrac=\frac{E_{\rm wall}}{\epsilon}
  \quad\text{a fraction, not a defect abundance.}
\end{keyresult}

\subsection{A local rate law off the background}\label{sec:outlook-local-rate}

\Cref{asm:geodesic} defines the inhomogeneous theory by requiring the transfer
to be uniform on comoving hypersurfaces, which makes the kinetic variables
diagnostic at linear order. Reading the rate law of \cref{eq:process-rate}
instead as a genuinely local law overdetermines the perturbations by one
condition, \cref{eq:perturbations-compatibility}, which the dust dynamics do
not satisfy. Two routes out are available and neither has been followed here:
allow momentum transfer, so that the donor is no longer geodesic and the extra
condition is absorbed by its acceleration; or give the receiving sector its own
dynamics, so that it is no longer an exact vacuum. Both change the linear
theory of \cref{sec:perturbations}, and the second changes
\cref{asm:closure}. Until one is carried out, the local reading of the rate
law is a statement about the homogeneous mode, and the hysteresis of
\cref{thm:hysteresis} is a property of that mode rather than of two regions
side by side in one universe.

\subsection{Caustics: what is now settled, and what is not}
\label{sec:outlook-caustics}

The clock congruence is pressureless and geodesic, so it forms caustics
\parencite{babichev2017_170403367}, and no linear analysis speaks to their
fate. An earlier version of this paper conjectured here that because the
transfer is proportional to the precursor density, and therefore diverges at a
shell crossing, the precursor would stay finite. That conjecture has since been
examined and is false: conversion enters the local solution only through the
integral of the rate, and the rate is bounded.
\Cref{sec:defects-caustics} states what replaces it, and carries out the
one-dimensional test proposed here.

What remains open is the self-consistent problem. The analysis of
\cref{sec:defects-caustics} prescribes the compression rather than solving for
it, so it establishes what a regular configuration would have to satisfy and
not that one exists as a solution of the coupled system. Settling that requires
either a self-gravitating sheet calculation with the transfer included, or an
existence argument for the self-similar profile; neither is attempted here.

\subsection{Spin and the Standard Model}\label{sec:outlook-spin}

The topological charge of \cref{sec:defects} is not a spin operator. Since
$\pi_4(S^3)=\Int_2$, a Finkelstein--Rubinstein quantisation in which the
soliton is a fermion is permitted,
%
\begin{keyresult}{S}{eq:outlook-spin}
  \pi_4(S^3)=\Int_2
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \text{the }\degB=1\text{ sector admits a fermionic quantisation,}
\end{keyresult}
%
but establishing it requires showing that
the $2\pi$ rotation loop is non-contractible in configuration space and
choosing a representation of the fundamental group. Even granting that, a
$\Int_2$ label is not a Standard-Model fermion: reproducing one would need a
covariant spinor sector and a mass matrix with mixing. Nothing in this paper
constrains either. The same applies to neutrinos: weak electromagnetic coupling
is not evidence of proximity to an absorbing state, and their free-streaming
affects structure growth in ways this model does not address
\parencite{lesgourgues2012_12126154}.

\subsection{Electromagnetism}\label{sec:outlook-em}

A phase alone is not a gauge field. Writing $A_\mu=q_*^{-1}\partial_\mu\theta$
gives $F_{\mu\nu}=0$ identically, so holonomies and phase singularities, while
real, do not by themselves produce the two propagating polarizations of
Maxwell. Nor is quantised flux the same as electric charge: a vortex can carry
magnetic flux with no net charge, and winding does not fix the value of $e$. A
condensate that breaks the relevant $U(1)$ gives the photon a mass
$m_A^2=Z_\Psi q_*^2 v^2/Z_A$, so a viable realization must preserve the
observed $U(1)$ or exhibit a different massless combination. Finally, the
degree-of-freedom count is an obstruction, not a detail: Einstein gravity about
Minkowski space has two helicity-$\pm2$ modes and Maxwell adds two of helicity
$\pm1$, and no invertible local change of variables in the gravitational sector
alone can create them.

\subsection{Averaged stresses and turbulence}\label{sec:outlook-turbulence}

There is a structural identity worth recording, provided it is not oversold.
When fast oscillations of zero mean ride on a slow background, the slow sector
inherits a stress quadratic in the oscillations: in hydrodynamics this is the
Reynolds stress $\avg{w_iw_j}$, and in general relativity it is the Isaacson
effective tensor $\avg{\partial_\mu h_{\alpha\beta}\partial_\nu h^{\alpha\beta}}$.
The two have the same form, and in both cases the mean sector acquires
something no individual mode carries. What follows from this is only that the
inherited tensor's anisotropy depends on the background, so it need not be an
isotropic bath. What does \emph{not} follow is a route from oscillations to
negative pressure, and this can be made quantitative rather than left as a
caveat. For transverse oscillations $w=a\cos(\xi\cdot x+\varphi)$ with
$a\cdot\xi=0$, the angular average gives
$\avg{w_iw_j}=\tfrac12a_ia_j$, hence
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:outlook-reynolds-eos}
  p_{\rm R}=\tfrac13\Tr\avg{w\otimes w}=\tfrac16\abs{a}^2,
  \qquad
  \varepsilon_{\rm kin}=\tfrac12\avg{\abs{w}^2}=\tfrac14\abs{a}^2,
  \qquad
  w_{\rm eff}=\frac{p_{\rm R}}{\varepsilon_{\rm kin}}=\frac23 ,
\end{equation}
%
exactly, and unchanged for any superposition of families with vanishing cross
terms. This is the ideal monatomic-gas relation, and it is not an accident:
$\avg{w\otimes w}$ is a sum of rank-one terms $\tfrac12 a\otimes a$ with
positive weights, so it is positive semidefinite and its trace can never be
negative. Measured instead against a total energy density including rest mass
one gets $w_{\rm eff}=\avg{v^2}/3c^2\ge0$, the warm-dark-matter result. Both
normalizations are strictly positive. An averaged quadratic stress of this kind
therefore cannot approach $p=-\rho$ by any choice of amplitudes or number of
modes; reaching it needs a different object, not a better average. The same
computation for high-frequency gravitational waves gives $p=\rho/3$. Concentration phenomena in the continuum limit are suggestive of
where a description of this kind would break down, but a singularity of a
continuum equation says only that the solution leaves a regularity class; it
does not say what replaces it, and it does not convert a singular region into a
stable absorbing state.

\subsection{Emergent quantum mechanics}\label{sec:outlook-quantum}

The Madelung and Klein--Gordon identities of \cref{app:quantum-hydro} are exact
rewritings of equations that were assumed, not derived. Writing $n=\abs{\psi}^2$
after starting from the Schr\"odinger equation adopts the quantum structure. A
theory of emergent quantum mechanics would have to produce the tensor product
of subsystems, superposition, entanglement and the Born rule, and would have to
explain why a two-particle state lives on a six-dimensional configuration space
rather than on physical space. Likewise, the semiclassical equation
$\Gmn=\Mpl^{-2}\avg{\hat T_{\mu\nu}}_{\rm ren}$ couples a quantum expectation to
a classical metric; it does not quantise the metric.

\subsection{What would falsify the programme}\label{sec:outlook-falsify}

Being clear about this matters more than any of the above. A specified version
of this model should be abandoned if it violates its own positivity conditions,
if it cannot maintain a conserved total tensor, if it develops an instability
in the regime where it is being used, or if it cannot describe the data with
its parameters fixed in advance. Three sharp tests already exist: the sign
prediction of \cref{thm:no-sign-flip}, the jerk bound of
\cref{eq:cosmography-jerk-bound}, and the opposite-sign growth signature of
\cref{eq:perturbations-growth-signature}. Having many parameters, or requiring
tuning, is not by itself a contradiction or a falsification; but it does weaken
explanatory power, and it obliges the statistical comparison of
\cref{sec:observations} to account for the added freedom.
