\section{Observational constraints}\label{sec:observations}

This section confronts the model with data. The framing matters: the aim is
not to show that the model fits, but to find out whether the sign prediction of
\cref{thm:no-sign-flip} and the jerk bound of
\cref{eq:cosmography-jerk-bound} survive, and to report the answer either way.

\subsection{Data and likelihood}\label{sec:observations-data}

\Cref{tab:datasets} lists the combination. Supernova distances constrain the
shape of the late expansion history; baryon acoustic oscillations tie it to the
sound horizon; cosmic chronometers measure $\Hub(z)$ directly and without a
distance ladder; and the compressed CMB likelihood supplies the early-universe
anchor. The absolute magnitude of the supernovae is marginalised analytically,
so the baseline combination contains no distance-ladder calibration; a variant
including it is reported separately.

Three choices deserve to be stated rather than buried, since each could
otherwise be mistaken for an error.

First, the sound horizon is computed from the matter density \emph{before}
activation. In this model the late-time matter density is lower than the early
one by the deficit $\Delta$ of \cref{eq:positivity-deficit}, and it is the
early value that fixes the acoustic scale. Using the late value would
misattribute the deficit to the early universe.

Second, the compressed CMB likelihood is a geometric summary derived within a
model family. Its use here is legitimate because the model's pre-recombination
physics is identical to the standard one, by \cref{asm:activation}: the
conversion rate at recombination is below $10^{-21}$ of the expansion rate. It
is not a substitute for a Boltzmann calculation, and the late-time-only
combination is reported alongside so the reader can see what the compressed
likelihood is doing.

Third, our semi-analytic recipe for the acoustic scale differs from a full
Boltzmann calculation at the few-tenths-of-a-percent level, which is several
standard deviations of $\lA$. Since every model considered here shares the same
early physics, the recipe is calibrated once at a reference cosmology and the
same constant correction is applied throughout; a test asserts that it is not
re-derived per model.

\subsection{Method}\label{sec:observations-method}

Posteriors are sampled with an affine-invariant ensemble sampler; chains run
until they exceed fifty integrated autocorrelation times with a stable estimate
of that time. Priors are uniform and identical across models wherever a
parameter is shared, so that the model comparison is not driven by prior
volume. Because $\epsilon=0$ reduces every absorption model to $\LCDM$ exactly,
the comparison is nested and $\LCDM$ sits inside the prior of each extension
rather than beside it. Details, including the exact prior ranges, are in
\cref{app:numerics}.

Two degeneracies are exact and are worth recording, because they mean certain
parameters are simply not measurable here. The branching ratio $\bfrac$ does
not appear in the background separately from the combination $\epsilon$
(\cref{prop:ide-map}) and cancels identically from linear growth
(\cref{thm:b-independence}); it is therefore held fixed. And the memory
parameters enter the jerk only through a factor degenerate with the rate
normalisation (\cref{thm:memory-fingerprint}), so they are constrained by the
shape of the expansion history rather than by its low-order derivatives.

\Cref{tab:comparison,tab:parameters} report the fits. Four things follow, and
the first is that the pipeline reproduces the standard answer: on the baseline
combination $\LCDM$ gives $\Hz=\numChainsLcdmBaselineDerivedHZ\pm
\numChainsLcdmBaselineDerivedHZErrHi\,\si{\kilo\metre\per\second\per\mega\parsec}$
and $\Omega_{\rm m0}=\numChainsLcdmBaselineDerivedOmegaMZ$, with the jerk
identically unity to the precision of the integration.

\paragraph{The convertible fraction is not measurable, for a reason.}
Profiling the likelihood in $\epsilon$ over $[0,0.6]$ never raises $\chisq$ by
more than $\numProfileXiHBaselineMaxDeltaChiT$ above its minimum, so the
one-sided threshold $\Delta\chisq=2.71$ is never reached and there is no upper
limit to quote. This is a statement about the parametrization rather than about
the data, and the mechanism is worth naming because it recurs throughout this
section: $\epsilon$ is the fraction available for conversion, not the fraction
converted, so the optimizer can always send the rate to zero and recover
$\LCDM$ exactly at any $\epsilon$. The same degeneracy appears geometrically in
the posterior. The vacuum present at activation and the vacuum produced by
conversion are two sources of one quantity, and the data constrain their sum,
not the split. The profile shows this in its starkest form: along it the
activation vacuum sits at the lower end of its prior, $\Omega_{V,\rm act}=0$,
for every $\epsilon>0$, with conversion supplying the whole of the present
vacuum; at $\epsilon=0$ it jumps to $\Omega_{V,\rm act}=\Omega_{V0}$, supplying
all of it itself. The fit is equally good at both ends because it is the same
fit. An earlier version of this analysis
reported a $95\%$ upper limit on $\epsilon$; it was the right-hand end of the
grid, and it is withdrawn.

\paragraph{What the data do constrain is the deficit.} The quantity with
physical content is not how much substrate could convert but how much vacuum
the conversion has actually produced, which is the matter deficit $\Delta$ of
\cref{eq:positivity-deficit}. Profiling it rather than $\epsilon$ gives a
genuine one-sided limit,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:observations-deficit-limit}
  \Delta < \numProfileXiHBaselineMatterDeficitLimitNV \qquad (95\%),
\end{equation}
with the profile rising to $\Delta\chisq\simeq\numProfileXiHBaselineMatterDeficitMaxDeltaChiT$
across the grid, so this is a constraint and not another flat direction. Barely
more than one per cent of the critical density can have been moved from matter
into vacuum since activation.

This does \emph{not} bound the jerk excess through
\cref{eq:cosmography-deficit-floor}, in either of the two ways one might hope.
That relation is a floor, so an upper limit on $\Delta$ says nothing about the
excess from above; and its hypothesis fails for the activation law used here,
for the reason set out in \cref{sec:cosmography-jerk}. The excess is therefore
profiled directly, as its own derived quantity, giving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:observations-jerk-limit}
  j_0-1-2\Omega_{r0} < \numProfileXiHBaselineJerkExcessLimitNV \qquad (95\%).
\end{equation}
Set against the $\sigma(j_0)\approx0.4$ of present late-time cosmographic
reconstructions, that is the honest statement of the model's current
testability: the signature of \cref{thm:jerk} is real, has the sign the model
predicts, and is far below anything existing data can resolve.

\paragraph{$\LCDM$ is preferred, and by less than it first appeared.}
\Cref{tab:comparison} gives the evidences. The extension is disfavoured, at
odds of a few to one rather than decisively. We draw attention to the size of
this number because an earlier version of this comparison, using AIC and BIC
with the sampled parameter count, reported a far larger margin; those criteria
are inapplicable here for the reason given in the caption, and both of their
errors happened to favour the conclusion we were drawing. The result is a null
one either way and is reported as such: nothing in this data combination
requires conversion, and the model's free parameters are bounded rather than
measured.

\paragraph{One chain is quoted from and three are not.} The $\LCDM$ ensemble
acceptance $\numDiagLcdmBaselineAcceptance$, length $\numDiagLcdmBaselineLengthInTau$ autocorrelation times
and is the source of the $\LCDM$ row. The chains for the extended models are
not, and this is a consequence of the geometry just described rather than of
insufficient running: with the rate parameters unidentified along the
$\LCDM$ boundary the ensemble spread to fill its prior in those directions,
after which most proposals fell outside the prior box and were rejected before
the likelihood was consulted, so the move rate \emph{fell} as the run went on.
Their posteriors are therefore taken from nested sampling, which integrates
over the prior rather than trying to walk along a degenerate ridge inside it.
The diagnostic that failed to catch this has been rewritten and now does;
\cref{app:numerics} records both.

\subsection{Reading the result}\label{sec:observations-reading}

Whatever the numbers, three things should be kept distinct.

A fit that prefers $\epsilon\to0$ would say the data are consistent with no
conversion, not that the mechanism is impossible; it would bound $\Gamz$ and
$\epsilon$, which is a useful and reportable outcome for a paper whose main
claims are structural.

A fit that prefers $\epsilon>0$ would not by itself be evidence for the
mechanism, because the model can partially mimic an evolving dark-energy
equation of state within the band of \cref{thm:sandwich}. What would
distinguish it is the combination of signatures: a positive jerk excess
distinguish it is the combination of signatures: a positive jerk excess
(\cref{eq:cosmography-jerk-bound}), a matter deficit of a consistent size, and the opposite-sign growth signature of
\cref{eq:perturbations-growth-signature}. Any one of these alone is degenerate
with other physics; together they are not.

And a fit that requires $\delta_{\rm eff}>0$ would falsify the one-way model
outright, by \cref{thm:no-sign-flip}. Current DESI-era analyses lean weakly in
that direction \parencite{kandel2026_260712283,yang2026_260821938}, which is why
the reverse channel of \cref{sec:positivity-track-b} is fitted alongside rather
than after the fact.

\paragraph{What this section establishes.}
Constraints on the model's parameters from public late-time data with an
early-universe anchor, obtained with code and checksums archived. What it does
not establish is a full CMB analysis or a nonlinear structure-formation
comparison; both are absent. The evidence is quoted together with its
sensitivity to the prior it depends on, which is the honest way to report a
quantity that has one.
