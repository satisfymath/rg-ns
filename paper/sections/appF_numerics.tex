\section{Numerical methods and reproducibility}\label{app:numerics}

Every number in this paper that comes out of a fit, a simulation or a solver is
produced by the archived code from public data and is not typed by hand: the
analysis scripts write results files from which the manuscript's macros and
tables are generated, and a build check fails if the two disagree, if a macro
is used that no results file defines, or if a decimal literal appears in the
prose outside a short whitelist. Round figures used for orientation --- an
order of magnitude, a quoted error bar from the literature, a fraction written
in words --- are not covered by that machinery, and are not results of ours.

\subsection{Background integration}\label{app:numerics-background}

Working in $\efold=\ln a$ and in reduced densities
$\omega_X=\rho_X/(3\Mpl^2 H_{100}^2)$, so that $E=\Hub/H_{100}$ and $E(a{=}1)=h$,
the survival identities \cref{eq:cosmology-survival} eliminate $\rhoI$ and
$\rhoD$ analytically. Only the three-vector $(\fconv,\Mmem,\omega_V)$ is
integrated, with
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:numerics-system}
  \odv{\fconv}{\efold}=(1-\fconv)\frac{\gamma}{E},
  \qquad
  \odv{\Mmem}{\efold}=\frac{\fconv-\Mmem}{\hat\tau E},
  \qquad
  \odv{\omega_V}{\efold}=\epsilon\,\omega_c\,a^{-3}\odv{\fconv}{\efold} ,
\end{equation}
%
and the Friedmann constraint evaluated in closed form at each step,
$E^2=[\omega_{\rm b}+\omega_c(1-\epsilon \fconv)]a^{-3}+\omega_\gamma
a^{-4}+\omega_\nu(a)+\omega_V$. Integration uses an explicit
eighth-order Runge--Kutta method with relative tolerance $10^{-9}$; the
solution is interpolated with cubic splines in $\efold$, which is the variable
it is smooth in. One background solve costs about $11$~ms.

The neutrino sector carries one massive eigenstate of mass
$\SI{0.06}{\electronvolt}$ and two massless ones with
$N_{\rm eff}=3.044$, using an interpolation that is exact in both the
relativistic and non-relativistic limits. Its pressure is obtained from the
exact consequence of separate conservation, $p=-\rho-\tfrac13\dd\rho/\dd\efold$,
so the cosmographic parameters remain analytic rather than
finite-differenced.

\subsection{What is checked, and against what}\label{app:numerics-checks}

The exact identities of \cref{sec:homogeneous-cosmology} are used as the
Friedmann closure, so they cannot also serve as tests of the solution. The
code therefore provides a second mode in which all five densities are
integrated independently, and the identities become genuine checks. The test
suite asserts the following, at every parameter point it exercises.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
  \toprule
  Check & Against & Residual \\
  \midrule
  Friedmann closure & the integrated solution & $\sim10^{-16}$ \\
  Survival identity \cref{eq:cosmology-survival}
    & independent five-density integration & $<10^{-10}$ \\
  Jerk & two independent routes (see below) & $<10^{-12}$ \\
  Snap & closed form versus differentiated $\jerk$ & $<5\times10^{-3}$ \\
  Total continuity & analytic $\dd E^2/\dd\efold$ & $<10^{-4}$ \\
  Solvable family \cref{eq:solvable-closed-form}
    & its closed form & $<10^{-9}$ \\
  $\LCDM$ limit ($\epsilon=0$) & an independent cosmology library & $<2\times10^{-5}$ \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The jerk check is worth describing because it is not a tautology. One route
uses the total-pressure identity $\jerk=1-\dot p/(2\Mpl^2\Hub^3)$; the other
computes $\jerk=1+3E'/E+(E'^2+EE'')/E^2$ from analytic derivatives of the
Friedmann constraint, in which the transfer terms cancel between the dust and
vacuum sectors. The two agree to machine precision, which tests both the
algebra of \cref{thm:jerk} and its implementation.

The activation exponent is checked directly: with $p=6$ the conversion rate at
recombination is below $10^{-21}$ of the expansion rate, whereas $p=2$ leaves a
rate nine orders of magnitude too large for the expansion-tracking form of
\cref{eq:solvable-activation}.

\subsection{Growth}\label{app:numerics-growth}

\Cref{eq:perturbations-growth-N} is integrated from $z=100$ with the growing
mode, using splined background coefficients so that $g_c'$ is analytic rather
than finite-differenced inside the solver. Massive neutrinos are counted with
the clustering matter. They are not sourced by the transfer, so including them
in the denominator of $g_c$ dilutes it slightly; at the redshifts probed they
are half a per cent of the matter budget and the effect on $g_c$ is of the same
order, far below the precision of the growth data. Treating them as fully
clustering also overstates their contribution on small scales, which only a
Boltzmann treatment would resolve.

\subsection{Likelihoods}\label{app:numerics-likelihoods}

Supernova distances use the Pantheon+ compilation with the full statistical
plus systematic covariance; the absolute magnitude is marginalised analytically
under a flat prior, which is verified against a brute-force one-dimensional
marginalisation. Baryon acoustic oscillations use the DESI DR2 measurements
with their covariance, and the sound horizon at the drag epoch is evaluated
from a fitting formula in the \emph{pre-activation} matter density, which is
the quantity the early universe actually determines. Cosmic chronometers use the Moresco
compilation with a covariance built from the published systematic budget: the
statistical errors plus the initial-mass-function and ``model, odd one out''
terms. That is the reduced budget, not the full one. The conservative variant,
which adds the stellar-library and full model terms, is implemented and tested
but is not what the fits use; it doubles the effective error on a coherent
offset in $\Hub(z)$, from $5.7\%$ to $11.6\%$. Reporting the fits under it is
left undone, and the choice is recorded here rather than described as ``full
covariance'', which is what an earlier version of this paragraph and of the
data table both said. The compressed CMB likelihood uses the shift parameter, the
acoustic scale and the baryon density.

Redshift-space distortions enter only the ``$+$growth'' combination and are
described here because an evidence is quoted from them. The data are the four
$\fsig$ entries of the SDSS DR16 consensus at $z=0.38$, $0.51$, $0.698$ and
$1.48$ \parencite{alam2021_eboss}, taken with the $\fsig$ rows \emph{and}
columns of the consensus covariance: at each effective redshift the growth
measurement correlates with the two geometric ones at $\abs{\rho}\sim0.4$, and
those are dropped because they overlap in volume with the DESI sample and
would otherwise be counted twice. The model is
$\fsig(z)=f_g(z)\sigma_8(z)$ from the growth system of
\cref{sec:perturbations}, and no scale cut is applied: the consensus values
are already compressed to a single number per redshift.

One choice there is consequential and is not marginalised over.
$\sigma_{8,0}$ is held at the Planck value $0.8111$ rather than treated as a
free nuisance parameter. The reason is that the question being asked is
whether the parameters the geometry prefers are compatible with the observed
growth, and a free $\sigma_{8,0}$ absorbs exactly the effect under test: the
model's signature is a change in the \emph{shape} of $f_g(z)$ together with a
change in amplitude, and an unconstrained amplitude removes half of it. The
consequence is that the evidence quoted for the ``$+$growth'' combination is
conditional on that value, and a reader who prefers to marginalise should read
it as an upper bound on how much growth data can help. We did not run the
marginalised variant.

One caveat is disclosed rather than buried. The semi-analytic recipe for the
sound horizon at recombination differs from a full Boltzmann calculation by
about $0.2\%$, which is several standard deviations of the acoustic scale. Since
all models considered here share identical pre-recombination physics, the
recipe is calibrated once at the reference cosmology and the same constant
correction is applied throughout.

How much that constant leaves behind is measured rather than assumed, because
a constant can only absorb a constant. Isolating the fitting formula for the
recombination redshift --- which gives
$z_*=\numCheckRecombinationRecipeZStarRecipe$ against Planck's
$\numCheckRecombinationRecipeZStarPlanck$ --- and recomputing the priors with
the Planck value shifts $\lA$ by
$\numCheckRecombinationRecipePosteriorMeanShift$ on average across the
posterior range, which the calibration absorbs, and that shift \emph{varies}
by $\numCheckRecombinationRecipePosteriorRange$ across the same range, which
it does not. Against $\sigma(\lA)=\numCheckRecombinationRecipeSigmaLA$ that
residual is $\numCheckRecombinationRecipePosteriorRangeSigma\sigma$. Over the
whole prior box it is $\numCheckRecombinationRecipePriorBoxRangeSigma\sigma$,
so the constant calibration is adequate only because the posterior is narrow,
and not in general. Two consequences follow and both are stated rather than
buried: parameter values quoted from the compressed likelihood carry that
residual, and it largely cancels from comparisons between the models fitted
here, which share $\omega_{\rm b}$ and $\omega_c$ at recombination and whose
posteriors in them overlap. We have not refitted with an alternative
$z_*$ to confirm the cancellation.

A test asserts that the calibration is not re-derived per
model. Users who prefer to avoid the compressed likelihood can run the
late-time-only combination, which is reported alongside.

\subsection{Maximisation}\label{app:numerics-maximisation}

The $\chisq_{\min}$ column of \cref{tab:comparison} is a maximisation, so it
carries a numerical error of its own, and that error has to be small compared
with the differences read off the column. Three protocols were used and the
first two failed in ways worth recording, because both produced numbers that
looked ordinary.

A fixed evaluation budget from a handful of random starts gave every model the
same number of likelihood calls regardless of dimension, so the three
parameters of $\LCDM$ and the nine of the memory model were searched equally
hard. Forty full simplex runs from uniform prior draws did find the maxima but
took $8.4$ hours for five models, because a uniform draw usually lands where
the background integration is stiff and the simplex then spends its whole
budget there. The protocol in use screens a Latin hypercube of
$\numMapXiHBaselineNScreen$ points once, refines only the best few --- which
are by construction in the cheap region --- and polishes the winner with
alternating simplex and direction-set steps until it stops moving, with a
budget of $\numMapXiHBaselineBudgetPerDim$ evaluations per parameter. The
alternation is not decoration: the simplex contracts across the curved
degeneracy between the rate normalisation and the activation redshift instead
of following it, and on the six-parameter model the direction-set steps
recovered $\numMapXiHBaselinePolishGain$ in $\chisq$ that the simplex had left
behind.

Two things keep the cheaper protocol honest. Nesting is exact, so a model
cannot fit worse than a model it contains; each search is seeded with every
submodel's best fit embedded, and a gate fails the build if the column is not
monotone in the nesting. That is not hypothetical: the nine-parameter search
once returned a value $1.55$ \emph{worse} than the seven-parameter model it
contains, which is impossible, and the number was on its way into the table.
And the slow run's answers are frozen in the repository as a ceiling. A
chi-square is attained at an actual point in parameter space, so a later run
landing above the frozen value has failed to find something already known to
exist, whatever its internal diagnostics say. $\LCDM$ reproduces the frozen
value to $10^{-9}$, which is what certifies that the likelihood itself has not
changed. A second gate fails the build on any larger gap, and it is also not
hypothetical: when it was first written --- the comparison had been described
in a docstring for some time without being performed --- it found the
reinforced model sitting $0.137$ above a value this repository had already
recorded, while reporting its search as converged.

What the protocol does not achieve should be said plainly. For the extended
models the agreement among independent refinements is poor --- for the
six-parameter model one restart in nine reached the best value --- and the
maxima sit on a prior edge in the unidentified directions, which is what one
expects when those directions are flat. The column is therefore the best value
the search reached, checked against a frozen independent answer, and not a
converged goodness of fit; the conclusions of \cref{sec:observations} rest on
the evidence column, which is computed by integration rather than by search.

\subsection{Sampling}\label{app:numerics-sampling}

Posteriors are sampled with an affine-invariant ensemble sampler using
differential-evolution moves, initialised in a small ball around a
multi-start maximum-a-posteriori estimate. Sampling stops when the chain
exceeds fifty integrated autocorrelation times and that estimate has itself
stabilised to two per cent. Chains are stored with the burn-in and thinning
actually applied, and the number of workers is chosen from available memory as
well as from the core count.

\paragraph{Why the extended models are sampled differently.} An ensemble move
is a difference between two walkers and knows nothing about the edges of a
prior box. When a posterior is wide compared with its prior in some direction
--- which is what happens here, because the rate parameters are unidentified
along the $\LCDM$ boundary --- most proposals land outside the box and are
rejected before the likelihood is evaluated. The rejection rate then
\emph{grows} as the ensemble spreads, so the chain decelerates and running it
longer makes it worse. Measured on the first attempt: cumulative acceptance
$\numDiagXiHBaselineDemoveFrozenAcceptance$, move rate falling from
$\numDiagXiHBaselineDemoveFrozenMoveRateEarly$ over the first tenth of the run
to $\numDiagXiHBaselineDemoveFrozenMoveRateLate$ over the last, and a fraction
$\numDiagXiHBaselineDemoveFrozenOutsidePriorFraction$ of replayed proposals
outside the prior at the default step scale --- against acceptance
$\numDiagLcdmBaselineAcceptance$ and no invalid proposals at all for $\LCDM$ on
the same machinery. The chains that produced these numbers are kept, so the
diagnosis can be repeated. Two changes follow. The sampler now works in a logit coordinate, so
no proposal can be invalid; this is a change of variables and not of prior,
with the Jacobian added to the target and a test that recovers a flat prior
under a constant likelihood. And the posteriors quoted for the extended models
come from nested sampling, which integrates over the prior rather than walking
along a degenerate ridge inside it, and which supplies the evidence used in
\cref{tab:comparison} directly. The convergence gate was rewritten at the same
time, because the version in place had no power to fail: it watched the drift
of a percentile pinned against a prior edge, which is insensitive to this
failure by construction and passes the frozen chains even now. It additionally
checks the move rate over the last tenth of the chain against the first, the
chain length in autocorrelation times, and whether any parameter has simply
filled its prior.

\subsection{Stochastic sector}\label{sec:numerics-stochastic}

The finite-population process is simulated exactly, not approximated. Because
the memory variable relaxes monotonically between jumps, the intensity is
monotone there, and two exact samplers are available: thinning with a tight
bound, and direct inversion of the integrated intensity, which for the
exponential rate law is expressible through the exponential integral. The two
are checked against each other with a two-sample test.

These simulations verify the deterministic limit of
\cref{thm:deterministic-limit} and its fluctuation scaling. With the rate law
of \cref{eq:process-rate}, the maximum discrepancy between the ensemble mean
and the deterministic solution falls from \numConvergenceLlnErrorNOZZ\ at
$N=100$ to \numConvergenceLlnErrorNOZZZZ\ at $N=10^4$, while
$\sqrt{N}\,\mathrm{std}(\fconv_N)$ takes the values
\numConvergenceScaledStdNOZZ, \numConvergenceScaledStdNOZZZ\ and
\numConvergenceScaledStdNOZZZZ\ at $N=10^2$, $10^3$ and $10^4$. That is
numerical evidence for the $N^{-1/2}$ law under the reinforced rate, and not a
demonstration of it: the exact binomial result of
\cref{rem:hazard-time-change} requires a state-independent rate, which
\cref{eq:process-rate} is not, and the three values drift upwards by about five
per cent over the range rather than sitting on a constant. Whether that drift
is a slowly vanishing correction or a different exponent is not settled by
three decades.

The hysteresis experiment of \cref{thm:hysteresis} is run in the same
framework: two histories are prepared to reach the same converted fraction
$\fconv=\numHysteresisFCheckpoint$ with memory states differing by
$\numHysteresisDeltaMemory$, and are then evolved under identical conditions.
Their asymptotic vacuum densities differ by $\numHysteresisDeltaVacuum$, a
relative difference of $\numHysteresisRelativeDeltaVacuum$. In the memoryless
limit the two histories coincide to solver tolerance.

\subsection{Defect profile}\label{app:numerics-skyrme}

\Cref{eq:defects-ode} is solved as a boundary-value problem on
$x\in[10^{-4},40]$ with a collocation method, using the trial profile as the
initial guess. The solution returns degree $\numSkyrmeDegree$ and satisfies the
virial identity $E_2=E_4$ with relative residual
$\numSkyrmeVirialResidual$.

\subsection{Symbolic verification}\label{app:numerics-symbolic}

Six analytic claims are verified independently with a computer algebra system
rather than by hand: the four-form stress tensor and its contraction
identities; the constrained-clock stress tensor and field equation; the FLRW
curvature tensors; the jerk identity; the reduction of the perturbation pair to
the second-order growth equation; and the closed-form solutions of the solvable
family.

\subsection{Availability}\label{app:numerics-availability}

The code, the data checksums, the chains' summary statistics and the results
file are archived at \TODO{Zenodo DOI}. Data are not redistributed: a script
downloads each public release and verifies it against a recorded checksum.

command, and the quality gates described above run as part of it.
