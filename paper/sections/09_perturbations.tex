\section{Linear perturbations}\label{sec:perturbations}

A background model with an interacting vacuum is not testable until its
perturbations are specified, and the specification is not free: an exact
vacuum has no rest frame of its own, so the way it exchanges energy determines
how it perturbs. This section fixes that choice, shows why the obvious
alternative is unstable, derives the growth equation, and corrects a result of
the draft version that is wrong when both products are present.

\subsection{The integrability condition and the geodesic realization}
\label{sec:perturbations-realization}

Suppose the vacuum receives energy with no spatial force in the frame $\umu$,
so that $-\covd^\mu \rhoV=\QV \umu$. Lowering the index, the left side is a
gradient, hence
%
\begin{keyresult}{I}{eq:perturbations-integrability}
  \covd_{[\mu}\left(\QV u_{\nu]}\right)=0 .
\end{keyresult}
%
This is a genuine constraint, not a definition: a scalar expression for $\QV$
does not by itself define a consistent transfer. Its spatial projection is
sharper than the Frobenius statement below, and is worth extracting because it
settles a question the geometric readings of \cref{sec:outlook} keep running
into.

\begin{proposition}[An exact vacuum fed without momentum transfer forbids
  vorticity]\label{prop:vorticity-obstruction}
  Let the vacuum be exact, $T^{\mu\nu}_V=-\rhoV g^{\mu\nu}$, let it receive
  energy with no spatial momentum transfer in the frame $\umu$, so that
  $\covd_\mu T^{\mu\nu}_V=\QV u^\nu$, and let $\umu$ be a unit timelike field.
  Then
  %
  \begin{keyresult}{C}{eq:perturbations-vorticity}
    \QV\,\omega_{\mu\nu}=0 ,
  \end{keyresult}
  %
  where $\omega_{\mu\nu}$ is the vorticity of $\umu$: the congruence is
  irrotational wherever the transfer is active.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The first hypothesis gives $\covd_\mu T^{\mu\nu}_V=-\covd^\nu\rhoV$, so the
  second reads $\covd_\mu\rhoV=-\QV u_\mu$. The left side is a gradient, whose
  curl vanishes identically, so $\covd_{[\mu}(\QV u_{\nu]})=0$. Decomposing
  $\covd_\mu u_\nu=-u_\mu a_\nu+\tfrac13\expa h_{\mu\nu}+\sigma_{\mu\nu}
  +\omega_{\mu\nu}$ and antisymmetrising removes $h$ and $\sigma$, leaving
  $(\covd_{[\mu}\QV)u_{\nu]}+\QV(\omega_{\mu\nu}-u_{[\mu}a_{\nu]})=0$.
  Projecting both indices with $h^\mu{}_\alpha h^\nu{}_\beta$ annihilates every
  term carrying a $\umu$ and leaves \cref{eq:perturbations-vorticity}.
\end{proof}

This is stronger than the clock argument of \cref{sec:realizations-clock},
which obtains $\omega_{\mu\nu}=0$ from $u_\mu=-\partial_\mu\clock$ and
therefore covers only that realization. \Cref{prop:vorticity-obstruction} uses
no realization at all. The consequence is worth stating plainly: a rotating
flow cannot be the one that feeds an exact vacuum, so any picture in which
vortical structures drive the vacuum production must abandon one of the three
hypotheses. The minimal abandonment is the second. Allowing a spatial momentum
transfer $q_\mu$, so that the source is $\QV\umu+q_\mu$, the same projection
gives $\QV\omega_{\mu\nu}=-h^\alpha{}_\mu h^\beta{}_\nu\covd_{[\alpha}q_{\beta]}$:
vorticity is permitted exactly to the extent that the momentum transfer carries
a spatial curl. That is a requirement on a mechanism, not a freedom. In the clock realization
$u_\mu=-\partial_\mu \clock$, and \cref{eq:perturbations-integrability} is
equivalent to $\dd \QV\wedge \dd \clock=0$, so that $\QV$ and $\rhoV$ are
functions of $\clock$ alone wherever the clock is regular. That proviso is not
decorative, and it is worth saying exactly what it excludes. The constraint
$(\partial \clock)^2=-1$ guarantees $\dd \clock\ne0$, so the only way the
Frobenius step can fail is at a caustic of the congruence, where the level sets
of $\clock$ self-intersect and $\clock$ ceases to be a coordinate. Caustics are
therefore not a remote nonlinear question about the theory in general: they are
precisely the locus on which \cref{eq:perturbations-com}, and with it every
result of this section, is undefined. We proceed on the assumption that the
congruence is caustic-free over the range of scales and redshifts probed by the
data of \cref{sec:observations}, which is a physical assumption about when
shell crossing first occurs and is not established here. On comoving
hypersurfaces of constant $\clock$ this gives
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:perturbations-com}
  \delta \rhoV_{\rm com}=0, \qquad \delta Q_{V,\rm com}=0 .
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Geodesic realization]\label{asm:geodesic}
  The theory is \emph{defined} by imposing
  \cref{eq:perturbations-integrability} with a geodesic clock, so that the
  volumetric transfer is spatially uniform on comoving hypersurfaces,
  \cref{eq:perturbations-com}. Equivalently, the local conversion rate
  anti-correlates exactly with the local precursor density,
  $\delta\Gam/\bar\Gam=-\delta_I$. The kinetic variables $(\fconv,\Mmem)$ are
  then \emph{diagnostic} at linear order: their perturbations are reconstructed
  from that relation together with whichever of their evolution equations one
  retains.
\end{assumption}

The draft listed four ways to extend the transfer off the background and chose
none of them. It is stated here as an assumption because that is the role it
plays in what follows --- every later result is proved \emph{under} it --- but
it is not an independent one: \cref{prop:geodesic-forced} below shows that the
hypotheses already in force leave no alternative to it, and the natural
alternative is in any case shown to be unstable.

\paragraph{The structural cost, stated plainly.} If instead the rate law
\cref{eq:process-rate} is read as a \emph{local} law, so that
$\delta\Gam/\bar\Gam=\alpha\,\delta \fconv+\beta\,\delta \Mmem$ holds
pointwise, then together with the two linearised kinetic evolution equations
one has three conditions on the two unknowns $(\delta \fconv,\delta \Mmem)$.
The system is overdetermined by one, and differentiating the algebraic relation
and substituting the evolution equations yields a compatibility condition,
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:perturbations-compatibility}
  \alpha\left[-\bar\Gam\,\delta \fconv
    -(1-\bar \fconv)\bar\Gam\,\delta_I\right]
  +\frac{\beta}{\tauM}\left(\delta \fconv-\delta \Mmem\right)
  =-\dot\delta_I ,
\end{equation}
%
which the dust dynamics do not satisfy identically. \Cref{asm:geodesic}
resolves this by taking the transfer condition as primary and demoting the
kinetic perturbations to diagnostics, which costs nothing in linear
observables: by \cref{thm:b-independence} the entire linear dust sector closes
on $\delta_{\rm m}$ and $\vartheta$ and sees the kinetic sector only through
the background quantity $\bar \QV$. It does, however, mean that at linear order
$\fconv$, $\Mmem$, $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\tauM$ are background bookkeeping
rather than local fields, so the locality of the rate law asserted in
\cref{sec:process-state} is a statement about the homogeneous mode. Whether a
genuinely local rate law admits a consistent inhomogeneous completion, and what
that completion does to \cref{eq:perturbations-com}, is an open problem; it is
listed as such in \cref{sec:outlook}.

\begin{proposition}[A clumping vacuum is gradient-unstable]
  \label{prop:clumping-unstable}
  If instead $\delta \rhoV_{\rm com}=\zeta\,\delta_I$ with $\zeta>0$, the dust
  experiences a force $\rhoI A^i=\partial^i \rhoV$, and the growth equation
  acquires a term $-k^2\zeta\delta/(a^2\bar\rho_I)$. The effective sound speed
  squared is then negative and short-wavelength modes grow without bound.
\end{proposition}

\Cref{prop:clumping-unstable} excludes one branch and not all alternatives, and
it is worth being exact about the scope, because two different questions are
easy to run together. \emph{Taken by itself}, as a statement about the growth
equation, it says nothing about $\zeta<0$, which gives a positive effective
sound speed; nor about the other completions the draft listed, namely allowing
momentum transfer with a compensating acceleration of the donor, or giving the
receiving sector its own dynamics and pressure. Those are different theories,
not refuted ones. \emph{Given the two stress hypotheses used everywhere else in
this paper}, the situation is different, and \cref{prop:geodesic-forced} below
is the statement of it: $\zeta$ is then not a parameter at all but zero, and
the compensating-acceleration branch is excluded rather than untried. The two
readings do not conflict --- the second assumes more --- but the first is the
one that survives if the recipient is allowed to stop being an exact vacuum. What can be said is that the geodesic branch is the one used
in the interacting-vacuum literature
\parencite{wands2012_12036776,sebastianutti2023_231214123} and the one for which
the linear sector closes. More can be said, and should be, because
presenting four completions as a menu understates how little freedom there
actually is.

\begin{proposition}[The geodesic realization is forced, not chosen]
  \label{prop:geodesic-forced}
  Let the dark sector consist of pressureless components with a common
  four-velocity $\umu$ and no heat flux, $T^{\mu\nu}_{I,D}=\rho_{I,D}u^\mu
  u^\nu$, together with an exact vacuum, $T^{\mu\nu}_V=-\rhoV g^{\mu\nu}$; let
  the remaining species be separately conserved, so that the dark sector
  conserves in total; let the transfer within it carry no momentum in the frame
  $\umu$, so that $\covd_\mu T^{\mu\nu}_V=\QV u^\nu$; and let $\rhoI+\rhoD>0$.
  Then, on any simply connected region where $\QV\neq0$ and the congruence is
  regular,
  %
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $a^\mu \equiv u^\nu\covd_\nu u^\mu=0$: the congruence is geodesic;
    \item $\omega_{\mu\nu}=0$, so $\umu$ is hypersurface-orthogonal and
      $u_\mu=-\partial_\mu\clock$ with $(\partial\clock)^2=-1$;
    \item $\rhoV$ and $\QV$ are functions of $\clock$ alone, hence
      $\delta\rhoV=\delta\QV=0$ on the surfaces $\clock=\text{const}$.
  \end{enumerate}
  %
  \Cref{eq:perturbations-com} therefore holds identically, and
  \cref{asm:geodesic} states a consequence of hypotheses already in force
  rather than a choice among completions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  (1) By hypothesis the dark sector conserves in total,
  $\covd_\mu(T_I+T_D+T_V)^{\mu\nu}=0$. Using
  $\covd_\mu(\rho u^\mu u^\nu)=u^\nu\covd_\mu(\rho u^\mu)+\rho\,a^\nu$ and
  projecting the whole equation with $h^\lambda{}_\nu$, every term carrying a
  free $u^\nu$ is annihilated --- including the vacuum source, which by
  hypothesis has no other part --- and what remains is
  $(\rhoI+\rhoD)\,a^\lambda=0$.

  (2) The first hypothesis gives $\covd_\mu\rhoV=-\QV u_\mu$, so $\QV u$ is
  exact and hence closed, which is \cref{prop:vorticity-obstruction}. With
  $a^\mu=0$ from (1), $\covd_{[\mu}u_{\nu]}=\omega_{\mu\nu}-u_{[\mu}a_{\nu]}$
  vanishes as well, so $u$ is itself closed and locally exact; the
  normalization $u\cdot u=-1$ is the clock constraint.

  (3) $\covd_\mu\rhoV=-\QV u_\mu=\QV\,\partial_\mu\clock$ says that
  $\covd\rhoV$ is everywhere parallel to $\covd\clock$, so $\rhoV=F(\clock)$
  locally, and comparing the two expressions gives $\QV=F'(\clock)$.
\end{proof}

One objection should be met before the consequences are drawn, because it is
the first a reader will raise: is the hypothesis not already the conclusion?
It is not, but the distance is short and worth measuring. The hypothesis is a
statement about the \emph{transfer} --- that its momentum part vanishes in the
frame $\umu$ --- and the conclusion is a statement about the \emph{congruence}.
What connects them is the donor's equation of motion, and the content of the
proposition is exactly that connection: two of the four completions the draft
listed, the geodesic one and the one with a compensating acceleration of the
donor, are not two options but one dichotomy, and choosing a transfer with no
momentum is choosing a geodesic clock whether or not one notices. A reader who
finds that obvious once stated has understood it; it was not stated in the
draft, and \cref{asm:geodesic} was written as though the two were independent.

The reason to state this separately from \cref{prop:vorticity-obstruction} is
that the two use different equations. The vorticity result uses only the
vacuum's own divergence and holds whatever the donor is. Geodesy comes from
the \emph{donor's} equation of motion: a pressureless fluid whose only source
points along its own four-velocity feels no transverse force, so it cannot be
accelerated, and the exact vacuum by construction exerts none. Once both are
in hand the third completion listed above, a compensating acceleration of the
donor, is excluded rather than merely untried, and the first, $\zeta\neq0$, is
excluded by (3). What survives is the fourth: give the receiving sector its
own dynamics and pressure. That is the one modification this paper's
obstructions keep pointing at, and \cref{sec:outlook-one-obstruction} collects
the others that point the same way.

\subsection{The branching ratio is invisible}\label{sec:perturbations-b}

\begin{theorem}[$\bfrac$-independence of linear growth]\label{thm:b-independence}
  Under \cref{asm:geodesic}, let
  $\delta_{\rm m}=\sum_k\bar\rho_k\delta_k/\bar\rho_{\rm m}$ run over all
  pressureless species $k\in\{I,D,{\rm b}\}$ and let $\vartheta$ be the
  perturbed expansion. Then for every $\bfrac\in[0,1]$
  %
  \begin{keyresult}{C}{eq:perturbations-system}
    \dot\delta_{\rm m}=-\vartheta+\gamma_c\delta_{\rm m},
    \qquad
    \dot\vartheta+2\Hub\vartheta=-\frac{\bar\rho_{\rm m}}{2\Mpl^2}\delta_{\rm m},
    \qquad
    \gamma_c=\frac{\bar \QV}{\bar\rho_{\rm m}} ,
  \end{keyresult}
  %
  and hence
  %
  \begin{keyresult}{C}{eq:perturbations-growth}
    \ddot\delta_{\rm m}+(2\Hub-\gamma_c)\dot\delta_{\rm m}
    -\left(\frac{\bar\rho_{\rm m}}{2\Mpl^2}+\dot\gamma_c+2\Hub\gamma_c\right)
    \delta_{\rm m}=0 .
  \end{keyresult}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Perturb the continuity equation of each pressureless species and add. The
  transfer contributes $-\bar\Gam\bar\rho_I\delta_I$ to the precursor and
  $+\bfrac\bar\Gam\bar\rho_I\delta_I$ to the defects, so the terms proportional
  to $\bfrac$ cancel in the sum and the residual transfer term is exactly
  $-\delta \QV$, which vanishes by \cref{eq:perturbations-com}. The
  $+\gamma_c\delta_{\rm m}$ of \cref{eq:perturbations-system} therefore does not
  come from the transfer perturbation at all: it comes from the denominator,
  because $\delta_{\rm m}$ is defined relative to a background density obeying
  $\dot{\bar\rho}_{\rm m}=-3\Hub\bar\rho_{\rm m}-\bar \QV$, which is being
  drained. The linearised Raychaudhuri
  equation with geodesic motion, and with shear and vorticity entering
  quadratically, gives the second, its source being the total dust.
  Eliminating $\vartheta$ yields \cref{eq:perturbations-growth}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:b-invisible}
  Every linear observable built from $\delta_{\rm m}$, $\vartheta$ and the
  metric potentials depends on the model only through $\Hub(a)$ and
  $\gamma_c(a)$. In particular $\bfrac$, and the split of the dark sector into
  precursor and defects, cannot be measured by any linear probe.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Correction to the draft.} Proposition 7 of the draft version wrote
\cref{eq:perturbations-growth} for the precursor contrast $\delta_I$, with
$\gamma_c=\bar \QV/\bar\rho_I$ and a gravitational source $\bar\rho_I\delta_I$.
That is correct only at $\bfrac=0$, and the restriction was not stated. For
$\bfrac>0$ it fails twice: the friction coefficient in the $\delta_I$ equation
is $\bar\Gam$, not $(1-\bfrac)\bar\Gam$; and the Poisson source is the total
dust, not the precursor alone, which by
\cref{eq:cosmology-survival} underestimates it by a factor tending to zero as
$\fconv\to1$. \Cref{thm:b-independence} is the repaired statement, and it has
the same form as what the draft wrote, but for the right variable.

\subsection{Growth, redshift-space distortions and the potential}
\label{sec:perturbations-growth}

In $\efold=\ln a$, with $E=\Hub/H_{100}$ and $g_c=\gamma_c/\Hub$,
\cref{eq:perturbations-growth} is
%
\begin{keyresult}{C}{eq:perturbations-growth-N}
  \delta_{\rm m}''+\left(2+\frac{E'}{E}-g_c\right)\delta_{\rm m}'
  -\left[\frac32\Omm+g_c'+\left(2+\frac{E'}{E}\right)g_c\right]\delta_{\rm m}=0 ,
\end{keyresult}
%
where $g_c=\omega_V'/\omega_{\rm m}$ in the reduced units of
\cref{app:numerics}, and the growing mode has $\delta_{\rm m}\propto a$ deep in
matter domination. Setting $g_c=0$ recovers the standard equation.

The observationally important point is that redshift-space distortions do not
measure $\dd\ln\delta_{\rm m}/\dd\ln a$. They measure the velocity divergence,
and here the two differ precisely because the dust is being sourced:
%
\begin{keyresult}{C}{eq:perturbations-frsd}
  f_{\rm RSD}=-\frac{\vartheta}{\Hub\delta_{\rm m}}
  =\frac{\dd\ln\delta_{\rm m}}{\dd\ln a}-\frac{\QV}{\Hub\rho_{\rm m}} ,
  \qquad
  \left[\fsig\right]_{\rm RSD}=f_{\rm RSD}\,\sigma_8(z) .
\end{keyresult}
%
The correction is not small, and its structure is the sharpest linear
signature the model has. Work to first order in $g_c$ during matter domination
and write $g_c\propto a^{p_g}$ near the onset of conversion. The exponent $p_g$ is not the $p_{\rm eff}$ of \cref{sec:cosmography-jerk}: that one is defined through $\mathsf q=\QV/(3\Mpl^2\Hz^2\Hub)$ and this one through $g_c=\QV/(\Hub\rho_{\rm m})$, and in matter domination the two differ by exactly $3$. Substituting one into a formula written for the other is an error of tens of per cent. The
effective exponent is not the switch exponent: since
$g_c=\bar \QV/(\Hub\bar\rho_{\rm m})$ carries an explicit factor of $\Hub^{-1}$,
%
\begin{equation}\label{eq:perturbations-peff}
  p_g=p+\tfrac32 \quad\text{for }\Gam=\Gamz\actS e^{\alpha \fconv+\beta \Mmem},
  \qquad
  p_g=p \quad\text{for }\Gam=\xi_0\Hub\actS e^{\alpha \fconv+\beta \Mmem},
\end{equation}
%
in matter domination, which we have checked directly against the numerical
solution. With that identification,
%
\begin{keyresult}{C}{eq:perturbations-growth-signature}
  f_g=1+\frac{2p_g+3}{2p_g+5}\,g_c+\order{g_c^2},
  \qquad
  f_{\rm RSD}=1-\frac{2}{2p_g+5}\,g_c+\order{g_c^2} .
\end{keyresult}

The two move in \emph{opposite} directions: density growth is enhanced while
the velocity field, and hence $\fsig$, is suppressed. The reason is that
$\delta Q_{V,\rm com}=0$ makes overdense and underdense regions lose the same
absolute energy density, so overdense regions lose the smaller fraction and
$\delta_{\rm m}$ is boosted, while $\vartheta$ is not. A model that removed
energy in proportion to the local density would suppress both, because the loss
would then be larger where the density is larger, removing the boost while the
drag on the velocity remains. Our numerical solution reproduces
\cref{eq:perturbations-growth-signature}, but the comparison has to be made
against the right reference and an earlier version of this paragraph did not
make it. \Cref{eq:perturbations-growth-signature} expands about matter
domination, where the unperturbed rate is $1$; by $z=2$ dark energy has already
pulled even the $\LCDM$ rate below $1$, so comparing the two rates to unity
there tests the expansion history and not the signature. Removing $g_c$ from
the growth equation while keeping the same background isolates it. At $z=2$,
for the parameters recorded with the check, the density growth rate is
$\numCheckGrowthSignatureFGrowth$ against
$\numCheckGrowthSignatureFGrowthNoTransfer$ with the transfer term switched
off, while $f_{\rm RSD}$ is $\numCheckGrowthSignatureFRsd$: raised and lowered
respectively, as predicted. The same holds at every one of the
$\numCheckGrowthSignatureNPointsTried$ parameter and redshift combinations
tried.

For the gravitational potential the relativistic Poisson equation gives
$\dd\ln\Phi/\dd\ln a=f_{\rm RSD}-1$, so during matter domination
%
\begin{keyresult}{C}{eq:perturbations-isw}
  \left.\frac{\dd\ln\Phi}{\dd\ln a}\right|_{\rm MD}
  =-\frac{2}{2p_g+5}\,\frac{\bar \QV}{\Hub\bar\rho_{\rm m}}<0 ,
\end{keyresult}
%
whereas in $\LCDM$ the potential is exactly constant then. The integrated
Sachs--Wolfe contributions therefore start earlier than in $\LCDM$ and add to
the late-time one rather than cancelling it. It is worth resisting the natural
shortcut here: one might expect $\Phi$ to decay at the full rate
$\bar \QV/(\Hub\bar\rho_{\rm m})$, since that is the rate at which the matter
is drained, but that answer holds only if $\delta_{\rm m}$ is frozen. Including
the back-reaction of the drain on the growth reduces it by the factor
$2/(2p_g+5)$, which for the fitted rate law and $p=6$ is about one
seventh.

\subsection{No large-scale instability}\label{sec:perturbations-stability}

Interacting dark energy models are notorious for a super-horizon instability of
the curvature perturbation, which afflicts couplings with a propagating dark
energy component and a constant equation of state
\parencite{valiviita2008_08040232}.

\begin{proposition}[Absence of the instability]\label{prop:no-instability}
  Under \cref{asm:geodesic} the system \cref{eq:perturbations-system} contains
  no independent dark-energy degree of freedom, no pressure perturbation, and
  no $k$-dependent coefficient. Its coefficients are of order $\Hub$ and
  $\Hub^2$, so $|\delta_{\rm m}|$ is bounded by a power of $a$ on every scale.
\end{proposition}

The instability arises when a dark-energy fluid is given a sound speed and a
density perturbation of its own while being coupled; an exact vacuum has
neither, and \cref{eq:perturbations-com} is precisely the statement that its
perturbation is determined rather than assigned. 
The scope of \cref{prop:no-instability} should not be inflated. The absence of a
$k$-dependent coefficient is the same fact as the vanishing scalar sound speed
recorded in \cref{sec:realizations-determined}: the scalar sector is degenerate
rather than hyperbolic, with no restoring force at any wavelength. What the
proposition establishes is the absence of the specific super-horizon instability
of coupled fluids carrying a sound speed, and nothing about the ultraviolet
behaviour of this sector or about caustics.

\paragraph{What this section establishes.}
A closed and stable linear system under \cref{asm:geodesic}, a theorem that the
branching ratio is unobservable in linear theory, a corrected growth equation,
and a quantitative prediction for $\fsig$ and the ISW amplitude. What it does
not establish is nonlinear behaviour, the fate of caustics, or a full CMB
calculation: the compressed distance priors used in \cref{sec:observations} are
a geometric summary, not a substitute for a Boltzmann code.
