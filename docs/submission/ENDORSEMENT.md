# arXiv endorsement — how to get one, and from whom

## What is actually being asked

arXiv requires a new submitter to astro-ph.CO to be endorsed by an established
author in that archive. The endorser is **not** vouching for the content. They
are confirming that the submitter and the work are appropriate for the
archive — that this is a research paper in that field and not spam. It takes
the endorser about two minutes.

Say that explicitly in the request. Researchers are cautious about endorsement
because they think they are being asked to approve the science; telling them
what the mechanism actually is removes most of the hesitation.

## Mechanics

1. Start a submission at arxiv.org. When it asks, choose **astro-ph.CO** as
   primary and **gr-qc** as cross-list. One endorsement suffices; the
   cross-list does not need a second.
2. arXiv gives you an **endorsement code** and a link of the form
   `arxiv.org/auth/endorse?x=XXXXXX`. It is specific to you and to the
   archive.
3. Send that code and link to the person you ask. They follow it, click, and
   you are endorsed. Nothing is sent back to you except the confirmation.

## Who to ask

Draw from the people whose work this paper is actually in dialogue with. All
of the following are authors of papers cited in `paper/references.bib`, which
is the honest basis for the approach: you are not cold-mailing strangers, you
are writing to the authors whose results you build on and, in two cases,
argue against. **Look up the current address on the institutional page or on
the arXiv listing for the paper** — do not guess it.

**Closest to this paper's own branch — the interacting vacuum with geodesic
cold dark matter.** This is the scenario Assumption 9.2 defines, and
Proposition 9.4 is about it. These are the most natural readers:

- **Marco Bruni** and **Natalie B. Hogg** — arXiv:2312.14123, arXiv:2002.10449.
  Hogg and Bruni have constrained precisely this scenario against data.
- **Matteo Martinelli** — arXiv:1902.10694, same scenario.
- **David Wands** — arXiv:1203.6776, the covariant formulation the paper uses
  throughout.

**Authors of the survey the paper argues with.** Section 5 quotes their
conclusion in full and then argues it is a property of the couplings they
surveyed rather than of the direction of flow. Writing to them is honest and
can only help: if they think the argument is wrong, better to hear it now.

- **Marcel van der Westhuizen**, **Amare Abebe**, **Eleonora Di Valentino** —
  arXiv:2509.04494/95/96.

**Adjacent, DESI-era dark-sector constraints.**

- **Weiqiang Yang**, **Supriya Pan** — arXiv:2608.21938.
- **Peter Dunsby**, **Saikat Chakraborty** — arXiv:2607.20348, on the jerk as a
  diagnostic, which Section 7 builds on.

**Chile**, for timezone and language: the cosmology groups at Universidad de
Chile, PUC, USACH and UNAB. Ask someone whose recent work is in dark energy
phenomenology; the department listings are public.

Ask two or three at once, spaced a few days apart. It is not rude — the first
to answer settles it.

## Draft request

> Subject: arXiv endorsement request — astro-ph.CO
>
> Dear Professor [name],
>
> I am an independent researcher in Santiago, Chile, writing to ask whether you
> would be willing to endorse me for astro-ph.CO on arXiv. My endorsement code
> is [XXXXXX], at arxiv.org/auth/endorse?x=[XXXXXX].
>
> I understand the endorsement confirms that the submission is appropriate for
> the archive rather than approving its content, so I will keep this short.
>
> The paper develops an interacting-vacuum model in which a pressureless
> precursor converts irreversibly into a localized product and a growing
> vacuum, at a rate that grows with the fraction already converted and with a
> lagged memory of it. It is confronted with Pantheon+, DESI DR2, cosmic
> chronometers and the Planck distance priors, and the result is a null one,
> reported as such: LambdaCDM is preferred over every extension fitted, at odds
> between 5:1 and 10:1 on the Bayesian evidence.
>
> I am writing to you because the work is closest to [your papers on the
> interacting vacuum with geodesic CDM / your survey of pathologies in
> interacting dark energy], which it leans on and, in one respect, argues with.
>
> The manuscript is at [link], and the analysis code and every generated number
> are archived at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22726266.
>
> Thank you for considering it.
>
> David Alexander Astudillo Muñoz
> daviddelmac@gmail.com
> ORCID 0009-0004-3589-1138

Keep it at that length. A long mail from an unknown sender gets skimmed; this
one can be read in thirty seconds and answered in two minutes.

## If nobody answers

It happens, and it is not a verdict on the work. Options, in order:

1. Ask three more people. Endorsement requests get lost.
2. Post the paper somewhere citable while you wait — the Zenodo record already
   gives the code a DOI, and the manuscript can be deposited the same way as a
   separate record of type *Preprint*.
3. Submit to JCAP directly. A journal submission does not require arXiv, and an
   accepted paper makes the arXiv endorsement automatic afterwards.
