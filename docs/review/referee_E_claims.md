# Referee report — claims, classification, and what the abstract promises

**Manuscript**: Self-reinforced geometric absorption: a memory-dependent kinetic
framework for vacuum, dark matter and localized structure
**Referee**: E (claims auditor: hypothesis/conclusion matching, evidential
classification, abstract-versus-body accounting)
**Round**: 3
**Date**: 2026-09-10

## Summary (3 lines)

A one-way, self-reinforcing, memory-dependent conversion law is postulated for
the dark sector, coupled to FLRW, and pushed to exact identities (positivity,
a de Sitter attractor, a jerk inequality, a b-independent growth equation),
three sign predictions, covariant realizations, a solved Skyrmion, a Lindblad
model, and a fit to Pantheon+/DESI DR2/CC/compressed-CMB that returns a null.
The mathematics I could check is mostly right and unusually well scoped; the
failures are of *bookkeeping between what was computed and what is asserted*,
and they are concentrated in the abstract, §10 and the Conclusions.

## Recommendation

[ ] Accept  [ ] Minor revision  [x] Major revision  [ ] Reject

I want to record at the outset that this manuscript is far more disciplined
than the median submission in this area: §§3.2, 5.3, 8.4, 9.1, 11.5, 12.4 and
13 contain retractions and self-limitations that most authors would omit. That
is exactly why the remaining overclaims must be removed — they are conspicuous
against the paper's own standard, and three of them are load-bearing for the
abstract.

## Major points

### M1. The paper fits a model it does not name, and claims to fit two it never ran

**Location**: `paper/generated/tab_parameters.tex` (entire file);
`10_observations.tex:90–96, 150–161, 63–70`; `05_positivity_ide.tex:235`;
`00_abstract.tex:46–48`; `code/absorb/params.py:8–17, 147–165`;
`results/evidence.json`.

Three separate failures, all in the same place.

(a) **`tab:parameters` is empty.** The generated file reads, in its entirety,

> `% Auto-generated by scripts/gen_tables.py. Do not edit.`
> `% chain not available yet`

Yet §10 says "\Cref{tab:comparison,tab:parameters} report the fits", the caption
promises "Posterior for the full model on the baseline combination", and
`10_observations.tex:158–159` states "Their posteriors are therefore taken from
nested sampling". No posterior for any extended model is displayed anywhere in
the manuscript. A referee cannot check a fit that is not printed.

(b) **The "full model" was never run.** The code registry
(`params.py:8–17`) defines `memory` as "Full model: self-reinforcement with a
causal memory variable" (9 parameters, adding `r_beta` and `log10_tau`).
`results/evidence.json` contains `lcdm` (3), `xi_h` (6) and `reinforce` (7,
parameters `omega_b, omega_c, omega_V_act, eps, log10_gamma0, alpha_tot,
z_act`). There is no `memory` run. So **β and τ_M — the memory sector, which
the paper repeatedly identifies as its only genuine novelty
(`01_intro.tex:66–70`: "What we have not found elsewhere is the dynamical law
… a memory variable") — have never been confronted with data at all.**
Meanwhile §10 writes:

> "And the memory parameters enter the jerk only through a factor degenerate
> with the rate normalisation (\cref{thm:memory-fingerprint}), so they are
> constrained by the shape of the expansion history rather than by its
> low-order derivatives."

A reader takes that sentence to mean the memory parameters were sampled and
came out weakly constrained. They were not sampled.

(c) **The reverse channel was never fitted, twice promised.**
`05_positivity_ide.tex:235`: "Both are fitted in \cref{sec:observations}."
`10_observations.tex:183–185`: "which is why the reverse channel of
\cref{sec:positivity-track-b} is fitted alongside rather than after the fact."
`params.py` defines `track_b`; `evidence.json` has no `track_b` entry and
`tab:comparison` has no such row. This is the one fit that would matter most,
since §5.3 concedes the data lean weakly the other way.

**Fix.** Either run `memory` and `track_b` and print `tab:parameters`, or
delete every sentence that asserts they were fitted and say plainly, in §10 and
in the abstract, which model was fitted. Minimum acceptable replacement for the
abstract sentence at L46–48:

> "The model is confronted with Pantheon+, DESI DR2 baryon acoustic
> oscillations, cosmic chronometers and the Planck distance priors *in its
> memoryless truncation* (β = 0); the memory sector and the reverse channel are
> not fitted here."

And for §10's method paragraph:

> "The memory parameters (β, τ_M) and the reverse-channel rate are not sampled
> in this work: the runs reported below are ΛCDM, the expansion-tracking
> benchmark Γ = ξH, and the reinforced law at β = 0. Fitting the full memory
> model is left to future work."

### M2. The abstract's testability claim uses an inequality backwards

**Location**: `00_abstract.tex:52–54`; `10_observations.tex:133–137`;
`07_cosmography.tex:57–70, 100–103`.

`eq:cosmography-deficit-floor` is

    j0 − 1 ≥ 2Ω_r0 + (9/2)Δ

— a **lower** bound on the jerk excess in terms of Δ. §10 then says:

> "By \cref{eq:cosmography-deficit-floor} that bounds the jerk excess below what
> present late-time data can resolve, which is the honest statement of the
> model's current testability: the signature of \cref{thm:jerk} is real and is
> out of reach."

and the abstract repeats it:

> "That bound puts the predicted jerk excess below the resolution of present
> late-time data, so the model's sign predictions are refutable in principle and
> not yet in practice."

An upper limit Δ < 0.0126 combined with `ε0 ≥ (9/2)Δ` yields **no upper bound on
ε0 whatsoever**. The inequality is being read in the direction opposite to the
one proved. The conclusion is rescuable, but only through the *exact* relation
in the same paragraph, `ε0 = (3/2)(p_eff + 3)Δ`, which requires the extra
hypothesis that q ∝ a^{p_eff} near today — and the margin it gives is not the
one advertised. With §7's own definition (q ∝ a^{p_eff}, q ≡ Q_V/3M²H0²H) and
the fitted activation exponent p = 6, p_eff = p − 3/2 = 4.5, so

    ε0 ≤ (3/2)(7.5)(0.0126) ≈ 0.14,

against σ(j0) ≈ 0.4. That is a factor of ≈ 3, not the "order of magnitude"
claimed at `07_cosmography.tex:102–103`:

> "while the model predicts $\varepsilon_0$ of order a few per cent: the signal
> is an order of magnitude below the present error bar."

"a few per cent" is nowhere derived, has no macro, and is inconsistent with the
paper's own 95% deficit limit saturated.

**Fix.** Replace the §10 sentence with:

> "The deficit limit does not by itself bound the jerk excess from above:
> \cref{eq:cosmography-deficit-floor} is a floor, not a ceiling. Under the
> additional hypothesis that q ∝ a^{p_eff} near today, the exact relation
> ε0 = (3/2)(p_eff+3)Δ gives ε0 ≲ 0.14 for the fitted activation exponent,
> which is a factor of about three below the current cosmographic error
> σ(j0) ≈ 0.4 — close enough that the test is not hopeless, but not a detection
> in any present data set."

and delete "of order a few per cent … an order of magnitude below" from §7,
replacing it with the same numbers. The abstract sentence must be rewritten to
match; as written it is an unearned reassurance in the direction of the
authors' own conclusion.

### M3. The Conclusions misreport a refuted conjecture as an unrun one

**Location**: `14_conclusions.tex:55–56`, against `11_defects.tex:151–204`,
`13_outlook.tex:48–59`, `fig:pancake`, and `docs/theory/06_*.md`.

The Conclusions state:

> "The clock congruence forms caustics, and whether defect formation regulates
> them is a conjecture with a test we have not run."

Both halves are false as of this version. §11.5 and §13.3 say the conjecture
"has since been examined and is **false**" (`13_outlook.tex:55–57`), prove it
(`thm:bounded-rate-obstruction`), and report the one-dimensional test that
§13.3 says was "carried out". This is precisely the failure mode the author's
own standing instruction is meant to prevent: a null result that exists in the
body is converted, in the section most readers actually read to the end, back
into an open question. Whether this is a stale sentence or not, its effect is
to hide a negative result.

**Fix.** Replace with:

> "The clock congruence forms caustics. An earlier version of this work
> conjectured that conversion regularizes them; that conjecture has been tested
> and is false for the model as published — a bounded rate enters the local
> solution only through its time integral and cannot beat a non-integrable
> compression (\cref{thm:bounded-rate-obstruction}). A compression-driven
> channel could, but only for κ>1 *and* b=0, which contradicts the b>0 required
> for a relic abundance; that tension is unresolved."

### M4. The Conclusions omit every negative result the abstract does report, and three negatives never leave §11.5

**Location**: `14_conclusions.tex` (whole section); `00_abstract.tex`.

§14 contains no mention of: ΛCDM being preferred; ε being unmeasurable; the
matter-deficit limit; the b = 0 versus b > 0 contradiction; the covariant
obstruction of `11_defects.tex:232–256`. Its "What has *not* been shown"
paragraph lists five items, none of them observational and none of them the
three structural obstructions. Conversely the abstract reports the fit outcome
but not the caustic refutation, the b-contradiction or the covariant
obstruction. The net effect is that a reader of abstract + conclusions — the
normal reading path — never learns of three of the paper's five genuine
negative results, all of which live in a single subsection, §11.5.

**Fix.** Add to §14, before the final paragraph:

> "Confronted with Pantheon+, DESI DR2, cosmic chronometers and the Planck
> distance priors, the outcome is null and is reported as such: ΛCDM is
> preferred over both fitted extensions, at odds of about five and eight to
> one; the convertible fraction ε admits no upper limit, because the rate can
> always vanish; and the one quantity the data do bound is the matter deficit,
> Δ < 0.0126 at 95%. Three structural obstructions are also left open: the
> earlier caustic conjecture is false; regularity at a caustic requires b = 0
> while a relic abundance requires b > 0, and no constant branching ratio
> satisfies both; and covariance forbids the compression-driven channel that
> would repair the first, unless an anisotropic-stress closure we do not supply
> is found."

Add one clause to the abstract listing the two structural obstructions
alongside the two "defects of an earlier version" already mentioned there.

### M5. "Every displayed result is classified" is not true, and "machine-checked" overstates what the checker does

**Location**: `00_abstract.tex:56–57`; `01_intro.tex:109–112`;
`appA_conventions.tex:63–74`; `scripts/check_claims.py:42–96`;
`preamble.tex:117–122`.

Abstract: "Every displayed result is classified on a four-level ladder … and
the classification is checked as part of the build."
Intro: "the classification is machine-checked as part of the build rather than
asserted here."

Three problems.

1. **It is not every displayed result.** `check_claims.py` enforces only that
   no bare `\boxed` appears outside a `keyresult`. Any displayed result that is
   simply an unboxed `equation` escapes. Among the results with **no ladder
   level at all** are: `thm:hysteresis` (the paper's flagship claim — the
   abstract's "memory produces hysteresis" rests on an unclassified theorem),
   `thm:quasi-positivity`, `prop:recipient-proportional`, `thm:sign-table`
   (whose sign table is quoted in the abstract as separating this model from
   running vacuum and CPL), `thm:bounded-rate-obstruction`,
   `prop:memory-origin`, `prop:no-rolling`, `prop:derrick`,
   `prop:clumping-unstable`, `prop:no-instability`,
   `prop:unitary-obstruction`, `prop:classical-reduction`,
   `cor:memory-invisible`, `cor:b-invisible`, `eq:solvable-exponent`,
   `eq:perturbations-compatibility`, `eq:perturbations-peff`,
   `eq:positivity-track-b`, `eq:realizations-suppression`,
   `eq:outlook-reynolds-eos`.
2. **The check has no epistemic content.** It verifies that a level *string*
   exists, is one of I/C/P/S, and that S appears only in §13. Whether a result
   deserves its level is asserted by the author exactly as it would be without
   the script. "Machine-checked … rather than asserted here" is therefore not
   accurate.
3. **The level is invisible to the reader.** `preamble.tex:117–122` typesets
   the environment as a plain `equation`; the level argument is discarded. To
   learn that, say, `eq:cosmography-jerk-bound` is C and not I, a reader must
   go to a table in Appendix A and match labels. A ladder that is not visible at
   the point of the claim does not do the job the introduction says it does.

**Fix.** Typeset the level as a marginal or right-flush tag on every
`keyresult`; extend the checker to require a level on every numbered theorem,
proposition, corollary and displayed equation that is cross-referenced from the
abstract, intro or conclusions; and replace the two sentences with:

> "Every displayed key result carries an explicit evidential level, printed
> beside it; a build check refuses an unclassified boxed result and any
> conjecture outside \cref{sec:outlook}. The check enforces the presence and
> placement of a level, not its correctness: the assignment is ours."

### M6. Two postulates are classified as "theorem of the model", and one numerical solution as "fitted to data"

**Location**: `03_absorbing_process.tex:62–65` (`eq:process-rate`, level C);
`06_solvable_early.tex:100–110` (`eq:solvable-activation`, level C, inside an
`assumption` environment); `11_defects.tex:90–93` (`eq:defects-solved`, level P).

`eq:process-rate` *is* the postulate of the paper. It follows from nothing; it
is the assumption from which everything else follows. `eq:solvable-activation`
sits literally inside `\begin{assumption}[Activation]` and is likewise stamped
"C = Theorem of the model". On the paper's own ladder, C means "Follows from the
new postulates together with the system specified". A postulate does not follow
from itself. This is the clearest instance of the thing the ladder exists to
prevent: the defining ansatz is displayed with the same badge as the theorems
derived from it, and the appendix table (`tab_claims.tex`) then reports both as
"Theorem of the model".

Separately, `eq:defects-solved` is the numerical solution of a boundary-value
problem with no data anywhere in it; level P is defined as "A number obtained by
fitting to data, valid within the stated likelihood and priors". It is not.

**Fix.** Add a fifth rung — **A, postulate/assumption of the model** — and a
sixth or a qualifier — **N, numerically computed result with quoted residuals**
— and reclassify: `eq:process-rate` → A, `eq:solvable-activation` → A,
`eq:defects-solved` → N. Also reclassify `eq:positivity-deficit` (a definition
of Δ, currently C) to I, and see m8 below on
`eq:cosmography-deficit-floor`. If the authors prefer to keep four rungs, then
`eq:process-rate` and `eq:solvable-activation` must be removed from the
`keyresult` environment altogether and displayed as plain assumptions, so the
appendix table stops calling them theorems.

### M7. `thm:hysteresis` carries a hypothesis it cannot satisfy, and the abstract restates it as something else

**Location**: `03_absorbing_process.tex:260–281`; `00_abstract.tex:11–14`;
`01_intro.tex:78–79`; `13_outlook.tex:43–46`.

The theorem's own conclusion reads:

> "Consequently, whenever the accumulated vacuum is given by
> \cref{eq:cosmology-dVdf} along a common expansion history, the two reach
> different asymptotic vacuum densities."

But by `eq:cosmology-dVdf` the two histories, having converted at different
epochs, already carry different ρ_V at the comparison time; by the Friedmann
constraint they then have different H. So the hypothesis "along a common
expansion history" is inconsistent with the conclusion in the coupled system
the theorem is invoked for in §4 and §7. As stated, the result is a theorem
about the *decoupled* kinetics with a prescribed a(t), which is exactly how
`fig:hysteresis` is computed — and that should be said.

The abstract then restates the hypothesis as something the theorem does not
assume:

> "memory produces hysteresis: two systems with identical densities but
> different memory states accumulate different vacuum energies."

The theorem assumes equal *converted fraction* f*, not equal densities. Equal f
gives equal ρ_I and ρ_D (by `eq:cosmology-survival`, for common initial data),
but ρ_V is path-dependent by the paper's own `eq:cosmology-dVdf`, so "identical
densities" is generically false at the comparison point. The intro is worse
(`01_intro.tex:78–79`, "two universes with identical densities but different
memory have different futures"), because §13.2 concedes the opposite:

> "the hysteresis of \cref{thm:hysteresis} is a property of that [homogeneous]
> mode rather than of two regions side by side in one universe."

**Fix.** State the theorem with its actual hypotheses ("two solutions of
`eq:process-deterministic` along a prescribed common a(t), reaching the same
f* at the same time with M⁽¹⁾ > M⁽²⁾"), and change the abstract to:

> "and that memory makes the kinetics path dependent: two histories that reach
> the same converted fraction with different memory states convert at different
> epochs and, integrated along a prescribed expansion history, accumulate
> different vacuum energies."

Also drop "two universes" from the intro in favour of "two histories of the
homogeneous mode".

### M8. "Derived rather than postulated" contradicts the paper's own disclaimer, twice

**Location**: `01_intro.tex:75–77`; `03_absorbing_process.tex:313–315`, against
`03_absorbing_process.tex:93–99`.

Intro, contribution 1:

> "whose memory variable is derived by eliminating a per-unit degree of freedom
> rather than postulated"

§3 summary:

> "A well-posed kinetic law with a controlled deterministic limit, a derived
> rather than postulated memory variable, and path dependence."

§3.2, two pages earlier, forbids exactly this reading:

> "Averaging a linear equation does not derive that equation, and
> \cref{prop:memory-origin} should not be read as doing so: it assumes each unit
> carries an internal variable relaxing towards its own state with a *common*
> time $\tauM$, which is if anything a stronger assumption than the population
> law."

The abstract gets this right ("exactly the average of a per-unit relaxation
law, so it is equivalent to a local assumption rather than an extra non-local
one") except that it omits the *common* τ_M — the hypothesis §3.2 identifies as
the load-bearing one.

**Fix.** In both places replace "derived rather than postulated" with
"re-expressed as the population mean of a per-unit relaxation law with a common
relaxation time, which is a local assumption of equal or greater strength
rather than a weaker one". Add "with a common relaxation time" to the abstract.

### M9. "Can only lose energy" and "cannot be tuned away" are true only of the one-way model, which the paper does not confine itself to

**Location**: `00_abstract.tex:24–26`; `14_conclusions.tex:34–36`, against
`05_positivity_ide.tex:214–235`.

Abstract: "prove that the effective dark matter can only lose energy".
Conclusions: "Three consequences are falsifiable and cannot be tuned away. The
effective dark matter can only lose energy, never gain it".

§5.6 introduces the reverse channel, notes it "preserves \cref{thm:quasi-positivity}
verbatim", states that "δ_eff can flip sign", presents it as the physically
natural counterpart in the four-form picture ("ordinary downward membrane
nucleation"), and claims it is fitted alongside. A property that a channel the
authors themselves endorse removes is not a property that "cannot be tuned
away"; it is a property of a sub-model. `thm:no-sign-flip` itself is correctly
scoped — the overclaim is only in the abstract and conclusions.

**Fix.** Insert "in the one-way model" in both places, and in the conclusions
say explicitly: "adding the reverse channel of §5.6 removes this prediction and
is a different model, fitted/not fitted here" (delete as M1 requires).

### M10. The N^{-1/2} claim is proved only for a rate the model does not use

**Location**: `00_abstract.tex:11–12`; `03_absorbing_process.tex:121–129,
144–194, 212–216`; `appF_numerics.tex:160–167`.

The abstract says "we show … that the finite-population process has a
controlled deterministic limit with $N^{-1/2}$ fluctuations".
`thm:deterministic-limit` states convergence in probability only; it contains
no fluctuation-scaling statement, and no CLT is proved anywhere. The exact
√N law is proved in `rem:hazard-time-change` (`eq:process-binomial`) — under
the hypothesis that Γ is state-independent, which the remark itself immediately
withdraws for the model in use:

> "The hypothesis that matters here is that $\Gam$ does not depend on the
> state; the reinforced law \cref{eq:process-rate} does, so the statement
> applies to a compression-driven channel and not to the reinforcement."

And `rem:residual-boundary-layer` shows the scaling fails outright for the
residual 1 − f, which is what ρ_I is proportional to. What supports the √N
claim for the actual reinforced law is a simulation (`fig:gillespie`,
`numConvergenceScaledStd*` = 0.888 / 0.907 / 0.933 across three decades — a 5%
drift the text describes as "within a few per cent"). The figure caption then
credits a theorem that does not contain the claim:

> "which collapse onto a single curve, confirming the $N^{-1/2}$ scaling of
> \cref{thm:deterministic-limit}."

**Fix.** Abstract: "…has a controlled deterministic limit, with an exactly
binomial N^{-1/2} law when the rate is state-independent and numerical evidence
for the same scaling under reinforcement". Caption: "…consistent with an
N^{-1/2} scaling, which is proved only for a state-independent rate
(\cref{eq:process-binomial}) and here observed for the reinforced law."

### M11. The "sharpest linear signature" is never confronted with the data that exists, and the omission is not disclosed

**Location**: `09_perturbations.tex:184–219, 265–271`; `00_abstract.tex:30–33`;
`tab:datasets`; `10_observations.tex:187–192`.

§9 derives `f_RSD` and calls the opposite-sign density/velocity behaviour "the
sharpest linear signature the model has", and the abstract sells it. §10 then
fits only distances and H(z): the dataset table contains no growth or RSD
measurements, and the closing "what this section does not establish" paragraph
lists a full CMB analysis and nonlinear structure formation but **not** the
absence of any fσ8 comparison. Compilations of fσ8 covering 0 < z < 1.5 are
public and the model's prediction is a one-line modification of the growth
equation the paper already integrates (`appF:78–88`). Either the comparison is
made, or the reader is told why not.

A second, related point: the whole §9 sector rests on `asm:geodesic`, which the
paper honestly calls a definition and not a derivation, and `prop:clumping-unstable`
excludes only ζ > 0 — §9 says so explicitly ("it says nothing about ζ<0 … Those
are different theories, not refuted ones"). The abstract's flat "the model
predicts enhanced density growth together with suppressed velocity growth"
therefore attributes to "the model" a consequence of a tractability choice.

**Fix.** Add a growth-data comparison, or add to §10's closing paragraph:
"No growth or redshift-space-distortion data are used, so the opposite-sign
signature of \cref{eq:perturbations-growth-signature} — the model's sharpest
linear prediction — is not tested here." And qualify the abstract: "under the
geodesic realization of the transfer (\cref{asm:geodesic}), which is a choice
of theory and not a consequence of the kinetics, the model predicts…".

## Minor points

**m1. `thm:jerk` states hypotheses the paper's own numerics violate.**
`07_cosmography.tex:13–16` assumes "pressureless matter, conserved radiation and
an exact vacuum". `appF_numerics.tex:32–38` integrates one massive neutrino
eigenstate of 0.06 eV, which is neither. The correction is numerically
irrelevant (I estimate |Δj0| ~ 10⁻⁷, below the 1.3×10⁻⁶ quoted for the ΛCDM
chain) but the inequality is advertised as *parameter-free*, so the hypothesis
list must either exclude massive neutrinos or carry the extra term with a bound.
Suggested: add "…and any further species whose pressure satisfies
|ṗ|/(2M²H³) ≤ δ, which for one 0.06 eV eigenstate is below 10⁻⁶ today".

**m2. Hand-typed numbers, against the paper's own guarantee.**
`appF_numerics.tex:4–7`: "no number is typed by hand". But
`10_observations.tex:150–152` reads "acceptance $0.44$, length $157$
autocorrelation times" while `\numDiagLcdmBaselineAcceptance` = 0.4386 exists
and no macro exists for the autocorrelation length at all. Similar hand-typed
values: 10⁻²¹ and "nine orders of magnitude" (§6, appF), σ(j0) ≈ 0.4 (§7),
"about one seventh" (§9), "about thirteen per cent" (§11), 8.3×10⁻² and
7.4×10⁻²⁶ (§11), 0.2% (§10, appF). Either promote them to macros or soften the
guarantee to "every number derived from the fits, simulations and solvers".

**m3. `tab:comparison`'s footnote points at text that does not exist**, and the
anomaly it hides deserves a sentence. The footnote reads "Not every
$\chisq_{\min}$ comes from the common maximisation protocol; see the text";
§10 says nothing about it. The anomaly: `reinforce` has χ²min = 1436.54, i.e.
**+0.02 above** ΛCDM, although §10's own method paragraph says the comparison is
exactly nested ("Because $\epsilon=0$ reduces every absorption model to $\LCDM$
exactly"). A nested model cannot have a worse maximum. Say in the text that the
extended-model χ²min is the best likelihood sample from nested sampling rather
than a maximisation, and that the +0.02 measures the sampler's resolution, not a
worse fit.

**m4. `p_eff` denotes two different exponents in §7 and §9.** §7 defines it by
q ∝ a^{p_eff} with q = Q_V/(3M²H0²H); §9 defines it by g_c ∝ a^{p_eff} with
g_c = Q_V/(Hρ_m). In matter domination these differ by exactly 3 (for the
constant-rate law, 4.5 versus 7.5 at p = 6). A reader who takes §9's
`eq:perturbations-peff` into §7's `ε0 = (3/2)(p_eff+3)Δ` gets an answer wrong by
40%. Rename one of them.

**m5. Unverifiable numerical claim.** `09_perturbations.tex:218–219`: "Our
numerical solution reproduces \cref{eq:perturbations-growth-signature}: at $z=2$
in a representative fit, $f_g>1$ while $f_{\rm RSD}<1$." Which fit? Which
parameters? What are the two numbers? No macro. Same for `:203–204`, "which we
have checked directly against the numerical solution".

**m6. The Conclusions misstate the activation exponent.**
`14_conclusions.tex:46`: "the minimal repair is an activation switch with
exponent $p>3$". `eq:solvable-exponent` gives p > 1 for the constant-rate law
— which §6 says is "the one fitted here" — and p > 3 only for the
expansion-tracking benchmark. Replace with "an activation switch whose exponent
must exceed the donor's dilution rate: p > 1 for the constant-rate law fitted
here and p > 3 for the expansion-tracking benchmark".

**m7. "Solves" is not earned.** `04_homogeneous_cosmology.tex:202–203`:
"which is the problem \cref{sec:solvable-early} solves." §6 repairs the defect
by *assuming* an activation profile whose two parameters §6 itself lists as
underived ("What it does not establish is a mechanism that switches conversion
on at a particular epoch"). Replace "solves" with "repairs, by assumption".

**m8. `eq:cosmography-deficit-floor` is level C on an unverified hypothesis.**
§7 itself says "whether the hypothesis holds has to be checked for the
activation profile in use rather than assumed". A conditional whose condition
is unchecked, and which is then used in §10 and the abstract (see M2), should
either be checked for the fitted profile — a five-line computation — or be
downgraded and marked "conditional on a hypothesis not verified here".

**m9. "Precisely the property lacking" overstates the comparison.**
`00_abstract.tex:21–24` and `14_conclusions.tex:27–30` say the donor-proportional

## Claims I tried hardest to break, and could not

I record these because a report of this length otherwise misrepresents the
manuscript. In each case I attempted a counterexample or a hypothesis gap and
failed.

- **`thm:quasi-positivity`** (§5.1). I tried to break forward invariance by
  making Γ singular, discontinuous, and non-integrable in a neighbourhood of a
  face. The proof survives because the argument is applied to the integral form
  and every outflow carries the donor's density as a factor
  (`appB:59–72`). The donor-versus-recipient diagnostic in
  `prop:recipient-proportional` is correct and is, in my view, the most useful
  single observation in the paper.
- **`thm:sandwich`** (§5.5). I expected the upper bound to fail, because the
  naive construction does. It does not: the split at t in
  `eq:positivity-sandwich-upper` gives two separately non-negative integrals for
  a ≤ 1, and the paper's own warning that the "obvious" bracketing by
  (ρ_m^early, V_early) is a *lower* bound is right.
- **`thm:no-sign-flip`** (§5.3) within the one-way closure. The algebra is
  exact, `cor:memory-invisible` is right, and — to the authors' credit — the
  section states plainly that current data lean the other way instead of
  burying it.
- **`thm:bounded-rate-obstruction` and `eq:defects-local-exact`** (§11.5). The
  exact local solution is right and the refutation of the earlier conjecture is
  genuine, not cosmetic: conversion enters only through ∫Γ, and ∫Γ ≤ G(T−τ₀).
  The three-condition analysis (g ≥ c for ρ_I, g > c strictly for the vacuum,
  b = 0 for the defects) is correct, and I could not find a fourth condition
  they missed. The follow-through — that the compression channel switches
  itself off after crossing, that the L^∞ topology is essential, that pointwise
  finiteness is not a singularity criterion — is exactly the right set of
  caveats.
- **`thm:no-go`** (§6.2), and its structural consequence. The observation that
  the *hypotheses* of `thm:regular-future` and `thm:de-sitter` had to be
  reorganised (0 ≤ Γ ≤ G rather than 0 < g ≤ Γ ≤ G, with ∫Γ = ∞ promoted to an
  explicit hypothesis) rather than their domains restricted is correct and is
  the kind of thing most authors get wrong.
- **`thm:b-independence`** (§9.2). The cancellation is real, and the
  explanation that γ_c comes from the *denominator* of δ_m rather than from the
  transfer perturbation is both correct and non-obvious. The correction to the
  draft's Proposition 7 is stated in full, with both errors named.
- **`prop:four-form`** (§8.1). I checked the ε contractions in (−+++) and the
  factor of ½; `T = −h²g + ½h²g` is right, as is the D = 4 polarization count.
- **`prop:no-rolling`, `prop:derrick`, `prop:unitary-obstruction`.** All three
  are elementary, correctly stated, and — unusually — correctly *scoped*: each
  is followed by the list of hypotheses that evade it.
- **The ε-unmeasurability diagnosis** (§10). The explanation — ε is the
  convertible fraction, the rate can always vanish, and ω_V,act compensates
  exactly in the profile — is correct, is confirmed by `results/profile.json`
  (ω_V,act ≈ 5×10⁻⁸ at every ε > 0, 0.327 at ε = 0), and the withdrawal of the
  earlier "95% upper limit" is handled the way it should be.
- **The replacement of AIC/BIC by the evidence** (§10, `tab:comparison`
  caption). The Davies'-problem argument is right, the direction of the earlier
  error is named as favouring the authors' own conclusion, and the headline
  margin is reported as having shrunk. This is the single most creditable
  paragraph in the manuscript.
- **§13 as a whole.** I looked for a level-S claim leaking into the body and
  found none; `eq:outlook-bridge` and `eq:outlook-spin` are the only S results
  and both sit in §13, as the checker requires. The disclaimers in §13.5–13.7
  (a phase is not a gauge field; the degree-of-freedom count is an obstruction;
  ⟨w⊗w⟩ is positive semidefinite so no averaged quadratic stress reaches
  p = −ρ) are correct and are the right way to close off the speculative
  extensions rather than leave them dangling.

The pattern is worth stating: the *mathematics* of this paper is more careful
than its *summaries*. Every one of my major points is a failure of §§00, 01, 10
or 14 to report accurately what §§3–13 actually establish. Fixing them is
editorial work on four files, not new physics — but until it is done the
abstract claims a fitted model that was never fitted, an upper bound derived
from a lower bound, and a complete classification that is not complete, and the
conclusions revive a conjecture the body has refuted.
